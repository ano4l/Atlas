# Atlas Should Match Maps, Not Install Hardware

Atlas's fastest route from synthetic-only positioning to a shippable product is to keep its step-and-heading PDR, wrap it in a particle filter constrained by its own corridor graph, and add absolute fixes opportunistically (QR checkpoints now, BLE and Android Wi-Fi later) rather than buying into any hardware-dependent technology. That recipe is what every competition-winning smartphone system in 2025 does, it needs zero venue hardware, and it realistically lands at **3–5 m median error with >90 % floor correctness** — good enough for mall wayfinding, where the IPIN competition still scores entrants on a 75th-percentile error with a 15 m penalty per wrong floor ([IPIN 2025 Track 1 annex](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/fileadmin/2025/IPIN2025_-_track_1_technical_annex.pdf)). On the data side, Atlas's existing map-package contract maps almost one-to-one onto Apple/OGC IMDF, PostGIS already contains the medial-axis primitive needed to auto-generate corridor graphs from walkway polygons, and the permissively licensed MapLibre stack (maplibre-react-native, Martin, tippecanoe, Terra Draw) can replace both the hand-traced SVG map and the empty operator dashboard. Data collection should copy the waypoint-click protocol used by Microsoft's and IPIN's benchmarks, store device-level trajectories only under explicit POPIA consent, and publish venue analytics solely as differentially private zone counts — the Dutch Enschede ruling (€600,000) established that any system *capable* of following a device across sensors over time is tracking, whatever the intent ([EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu/news/national-news/2021/dutch-dpa-fines-municipality-wi-fi-tracking_es)). Commercially, the 2026 landscape is consolidating around map-first vendors with unpublished per-square-metre pricing, several positioning vendors have abandoned malls for healthcare and workplaces, and South African flagship malls already run kiosk/QR wayfinding with no blue-dot app — which means Atlas's defensible assets are owned map data, a positioning engine that works on mid-tier Samsung Androids (82 % of SA phones), and intent data that passive footfall sensors cannot see.

## Map-matched PDR with opportunistic radio reaches 3–5 m without venue hardware

The state of the art for a consumer phone in a mall has not changed in kind since 2020, only in packaging: classic step detection plus gyro heading remains the motion model, and the winners wrap it in a particle filter with map constraints and correct it with whatever absolute source is available. The sole onsite competitor at IPIN 2025 Track 1 (THWS & cronn) ran a modular CONDENSATION particle filter fusing BLE/Wi-Fi with step and turn detection to survive radio outages, in real time on the phone; the Track 3 entrant from CUMT stacked PDR, Wi-Fi/BLE fingerprinting, a CNN floor classifier and map-constrained graph optimisation ([IPIN 2025 competitors](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/2025/competitors)). The value of the map constraint is large: one recent study reports mean PDR error of **2.97 m on 238 m paths falling to 0.35 m** once a map-matching particle filter is applied, though that is a single study and should be read as directional rather than as a target ([JRSGR](https://jrsgr.sru.ac.ir/article_12603_e4a8bff6c70e416428e7568371bc83a5.pdf)). Learned inertial odometry (RoNIN, TLIO) is open source but research-grade: RoNIN is GPL-3.0 with half its dataset withheld ([Sachini/ronin](https://github.com/Sachini/ronin)), TLIO's data is CC-BY-NC ([CathIAS/TLIO](https://github.com/CathIAS/TLIO)), and even 2025 successors concede that data-driven methods "often suffer from significant drift under complex nonlinear motion patterns (e.g., turns)" ([StarIO](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16121v2)). Their real advantage is pocket/bag carriage where no steps are visible; for a handheld navigation app the gain over good step-PDR is modest, so Atlas should treat learned models as a later refinement, not a foundation.

The absolute-fix options divide sharply by platform. **Wi-Fi RTT is Android-only and enterprise-AP-only**: Google documents 1–2 m accuracy with three or more responders, but the supported AP list is Cisco Catalyst 91xx/CW91xx, Aruba 5xx/6xx/7xx and Google Nest ([Android Wi-Fi RTT guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifi-rtt)), and a September 2026 study on a live campus WLAN found a **1.21 m median and 2.68 m 90th percentile** only after per-AP calibration, because "a single global correction is insufficient" ([arXiv 2609.13795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13795)). iOS exposes no Wi-Fi scan API at all — apps see only the connected SSID, and private frameworks mean App Store rejection ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/65129)) — and Android throttles RSSI scans to four per two minutes in the foreground ([Android Wi-Fi scanning](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifi-scan)). Wi-Fi is therefore a slow Android-only corrector behind a capability check, never the cross-platform story. **BLE is the only radio that works on both platforms**, but it is owner-installed infrastructure: academic density figures give **<2.6 m at 95 % with one beacon per 30 m² and <4.8 m with one per 100 m²** ([ISC](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/3602168.htm)), and vendors quote 1–5 beacons per 150 m² ([Mapsted](https://mapsted.com/indoor-location-technologies/ble-positioning)). Corridor-graph matching absorbs lateral error, so a sparse deployment (roughly 7–10 beacons per 1,000 m² of corridor) can deliver zone-level fixes that reset PDR drift without a fingerprint survey. The newer radio standards are not yet real: Nordic states that smartphones do not support Bluetooth direction finding ([Nordic](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/Low-power-short-range-wireless/Direction-finding)); Channel Sounding reached only the iPhone 17 lineup with iOS 27 and requires Bluetooth 6.3 accessories ([Novel Bits](https://newsletter.novelbits.io/archive/ios-27-brings-channel-sounding-to-the-iphone/)); and phone UWB is a peer-ranging API that needs an out-of-band BLE handshake per anchor, foreground execution, and flagship hardware ([Android UWB guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/uwb)). Android 16's unified `RangingManager` (UWB, BLE CS, Wi-Fi RTT, BLE RSSI) is the API to watch for 2027+, not to build on today ([RangingManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/ranging/RangingManager)).

Two zero-infrastructure sources deserve more attention than radio. Phone visual-inertial odometry is a far better motion model than step counting whenever the camera is up: ARKit drifted about **0.02 m per second** against an OptiTrack reference in a four-system benchmark ([arXiv 2207.06780](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.06780)), although the ADVIO mall sequences showed 95th-percentile errors near 10 m over long walks and ARCore failures from wrong loop closures ([ADVIO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09828)), so VIO still needs the same checkpoint and map anchoring. Absolute visual positioning is not within reach: Google's VPS covers only Street-View-imaged areas ([Geospatial](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/geospatial)), and the open stacks are server tools — hloc is Apache-2.0 but GPU/Python with no mobile support ([hloc](https://github.com/cvg/Hierarchical-Localization)), ORB-SLAM3 is GPL-3.0 and "not optimized for mobile applications" ([arXiv 2605.18385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18385)). Magnetic fingerprinting is genuinely infrastructure-free and used by IndoorAtlas, Oriient and Mapsted, but IndoorAtlas itself now quotes **2–5 m typical accuracy** ([IndoorAtlas support](https://support.indooratlas.com/support/solutions/articles/36000035912-what-is-the-positioning-accuracy-of-indooratlas-)), cross-device offsets are a known problem ([Magil](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~gchan/papers/ToSN19_magil.pdf)), and no maintained open-source phone library exists. Its right role in Atlas is a per-edge 1-D magnetic profile used as an extra likelihood term to disambiguate parallel corridors and detect elevators. Floor detection should likewise be relative rather than absolute: Microsoft Research showed floor heights of 2.5–6 m sit inside phone-barometer error, while pressure-change *rate* cleanly separates stairs, escalators and elevators ([MSR HotMobile14](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hotmobile14b.pdf)), and a 2019 multi-phone study achieved >85 % floor accuracy this way ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/6ebd8331ae044757a707e739add3d7e6)). Atlas already has vertical connector nodes in its graph; gating pressure events to those nodes and re-zeroing the baseline on any confirmed fix is the cheapest high-value positioning improvement available.

| Technique | Owner hardware | Platforms | Realistic 2026 accuracy | Role for Atlas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PDR + corridor-graph particle filter | None | Both | 3–5 m median with fixes; drifts alone | Core engine |
| ARKit/ARCore VIO as motion model | None | Both | ~0.02 m/s drift; fails in featureless areas | Camera-up / AR mode |
| QR checkpoints | Printed codes | Both | Exact at scan | Ground truth and in-app reset |
| Barometer transition gating | None | Both | >85 % floor accuracy | Floor engine |

| Wi-Fi RTT 802.11mc/az | Only if venue WLAN is Cisco/Aruba FTM | Android 9+ | ~1.2 m median (calibrated) | Opportunistic bonus |
| Wi-Fi RSSI fingerprint | None (survey) | Android, 4 scans/2 min | 2–5 m | Slow corrector |
| UWB / BLE AoA / Channel Sounding | Anchors | Flagships only | 0.1–0.3 m | Not before 2027 |
| Open VPS (hloc) | Venue scan + GPU server | Both | Sub-metre when localised | Not for a solo team |

Among open frameworks, OpenHPS is the only TypeScript-native fusion graph with a React Native add-on and an Apache-2.0 licence, but at 35 stars and essentially one academic maintainer it is a dependency risk; its node/graph patterns are worth borrowing rather than importing ([OpenHPS](https://github.com/OpenHPS/openhps-core)). FIND3 (MIT, Go) and Anyplace (MIT, Scala server with Kotlin Android libs) are readable reference implementations for fingerprint storage and kNN matching on the Kotlin backend ([FIND3](https://github.com/schollz/find3); [Anyplace](https://github.com/dmsl/anyplace)). Atlas should adopt IPIN's metric set — third-quartile point error plus a 15 m floor penalty — for its own replay evaluation so that synthetic-to-real results are comparable with the literature; the best public mall-scale number to calibrate expectations against is the Microsoft Indoor Location Competition 2.0 winner's **1.50 m mean error**, achieved offline with post-processing across 1,170 teams ([Hu et al., MobiCom 2023](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/the-wisdom-of-1170-teams-lessons-and-experiences-from-a-large-ind/)); online, on-phone systems should expect two to three times that.

## IMDF plus PostGIS medial axis replaces hand tracing

Atlas's map package — floors, POIs, and corridor-graph edges carrying step-free, one-way and schedule attributes — already mirrors the concepts of IMDF, the GeoJSON-based format Apple contributed to OGC as a royalty-free Community Standard in 2021 ([OGC IMDF](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/index.html)). Level maps to floor, Unit/Occupant/Amenity to POI, Opening to door node, and `Relationship` features with `direction: directed` and `hours` to one-way or scheduled escalator edges ([OGC Relationship](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/Relationship/index.html)); Unit and Opening `accessibility: ["wheelchair"]` arrays encode step-free routing ([OGC Unit](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/Unit/index.html)). The decisive detail is that IMDF explicitly does *not* carry a routing network — curated paths are "hints" and "not meant for a comprehensive routing network" — and neither does OSM's Simple Indoor Tagging, which has no routing section at all ([OSM SIT](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_Indoor_Tagging)). OGC's IndoorGML 2.0 is the only standard built around a navigation graph, but Part 1 (conceptual model) was published only in August 2025 and its JSON encoding is still unratified ([OGC](https://www.ogc.org/ogc-publishes-indoorgml-2-0-part-1-conceptual-model-standard/); [ISPRS 2026](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/L-4-W1-2026/87/2026/)). The implication is that Atlas's owned corridor graph *is* the differentiator; IMDF should be the import/export adapter that lets venue teams and Mappedin/FME-based workflows exchange data with it, while the graph stays proprietary.

OpenStreetMap is a schema source, not a data source, for this market. Taginfo shows over 700 million building objects against roughly **one million indoor objects worldwide** ([SotM 2026](https://pretalx.com/sotm2026-osm-science/talk/CTNPQJ/)), and an Overpass query of the Sandton City bounding box on 14 November 2026 returned **eight `indoor=*` ways, zero nodes, zero relations** ([Overpass](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter?data=%5Bout%3Ajson%5D%5Btimeout%3A25%5D%3Bnwr%5B%22indoor%22%5D%28-26.112%2C28.048%2C-26.102%2C28.062%29%3Bout%20count%3B)). Mainstream routers are no help either: GraphHopper states its level handling is "not for handling indoor routing across different floors" ([GraphHopper forum](https://discuss.graphhopper.com/t/multi-floor-support/2398)), and only OpenTripPlanner routes `highway=elevator` nodes across levels ([OTP wiki](https://github.com/opentripplanner/OpenTripPlanner/wiki/Indoor-mapping)). What OSM does offer is a stable vocabulary (SIT's `indoor=corridor`, `level`, `conveying=yes`) and the indoor= vector-tile schema (`area.class`, `area.level`, `poi.level`, `transportation.conveying`) that Atlas should borrow for layer naming so the same tiles drive both the web dashboard and the phone ([indoorequal schema](https://indoorequal.com/doc/schema)). Adopting SIT-compatible tags internally also unlocks JOSM IndoorHelper and OsmInEdit (ISC) as free authoring UIs on private files, plus `osmtoimdf` for export ([OsmInEdit](https://github.com/PanierAvide/OsmInEdit); [osmtoimdf](https://github.com/danielrotaermel/osmtoimdf)).

There is no open-source "Mappedin-style" venue editor to adopt. OpenIndoorMaps is Apache-2.0 TypeScript on MapLibre but pre-alpha with its editor "planned, not yet available" ([OpenIndoorMaps](https://github.com/openindoormaps/openindoormaps)); indrz is a Django/Vue stack whose business model depends on the maintainer's paid hosting ([indrz](https://github.com/indrz/indrz)). The pragmatic path for the empty Next.js dashboard is **MapLibre GL JS plus Terra Draw** (MIT, 1.1k stars, OSGeo community project, with MapLibre v4/5 adapters and a select/edit mode) over a georeferenced floor-plan raster served as an image source — assisted tracing with snapping, not full automation ([Terra Draw](https://github.com/JamesLMilner/terra-draw)). Floor-plan vectorisation ML will not pay off in 2026 for malls: CubiCasa5k's 5,000 plans are residential CAD drawings and its reference code is pinned to PyTorch 1.0 ([CubiCasa5k](https://github.com/CubiCasa/CubiCasa5k)); RoomFormer consumes 3D point-cloud density maps, not raster plans ([RoomFormer](https://github.com/ywyue/RoomFormer)); and mall lease plans show tenant blocks rather than walls. Phone LiDAR is a ground-truthing aid for specific areas — Apple RoomPlan exposes walls and doors as parametric objects with no official 2D export ([Apple CapturedRoom](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom.md)), and Polycam's DXF/SVG floor-plan export requires a Business plan ([Polycam](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/27756102599572)). Floor plans themselves carry thin but real copyright, and rendered mall maps are independently copyrightable ([Phelps](https://phelps.com/insights/courthouse-reporter-series-two-recent-cases-address-copyright-protection-for-architectural-works.html); [OSM legal-talk](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/legal-talk/2023-January/009186.html)); the workable contract is a written licence from the mall operator to use CAD or lease plans as a *source* and publish a *derived* vector map, with Atlas owning the derived graph — a mapper who asked mall managements for exactly this reported they "nearly always accepted" ([OSM help](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/32198/floor-plans-copyright/)). Never trace a competitor kiosk's rendered map.

Graph generation is the one place where the Kotlin/PostGIS backend already has the hard primitive. `CG_ApproximateMedialAxis`, available from PostGIS 3.5 with the SFCGAL backend, derives a pruned straight-skeleton centreline from walkway polygons ([PostGIS docs](https://postgis.net/docs/CG_ApproximateMedialAxis.html)); noding that output, snapping door midpoints and POI entrances to the nearest skeleton edge, and inserting vertical connector edges with `step_free`, `one_way` and `schedule` attributes yields a pgRouting edge table on which `pgr_dijkstra`'s inner query filters out closed or non-step-free edges at query time ([pgRouting concepts](https://access.crunchydata.com/documentation/pgrouting/3.7.1/pgRouting-concepts.html)). The TypeScript `straight-skeleton` package (MIT, CGAL compiled to WebAssembly) lets the editor preview generated corridors live before committing ([straight-skeleton](https://github.com/StrandedKitty/straight-skeleton)), with the caveat that CGAL mixes GPL and LGPL packages and the effective licence of the embedded code must be checked before shipping it in proprietary binaries ([CGAL licence](https://www.cgal.org/license.html)). Skeletons zig-zag in wide atria, so a hybrid — skeleton for corridors, visibility edges inside large open units — is the natural follow-on. For rendering, the whole stack is permissive and current: `@maplibre/maplibre-react-native` v11 is MIT, New-Architecture-only, Expo-compatible, and released v11.5.1 on 29 October 2026 ([releases](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-react-native/releases)); Martin (Rust, MIT/Apache-2.0, 4.1k stars) auto-discovers PostGIS tables and SQL function sources and hit 1.0 in November 2025 ([Martin](https://github.com/maplibre/martin)); tippecanoe (BSD-2, maintained by Felt) bakes each venue's GeoJSON into a PMTiles file for the offline venue cache ([tippecanoe](https://github.com/felt/tippecanoe)). Floor switching is a `level` property on every feature plus style filters, exactly as indoorequal and map-gl-indoor do it ([maplibre-gl-indoorequal](https://github.com/indoorequal/maplibre-gl-indoorequal)). The SVG renderer remains acceptable for one small venue, but loses on labelling, rotation, tile caching and 3D once a second mall arrives.

## Checkpoint-anchored walks and DP zone counts keep Atlas inside POPIA

Atlas's QR checkpoints are functionally identical to the ground-truth protocol used by every major benchmark. Microsoft's Indoor Location Competition 2.0 had a site surveyor walk with the phone held flat and tap pre-surveyed waypoints, logged as `TYPE_WAYPOINT` rows in floor-plan metres alongside `TYPE_WIFI` and `TYPE_BEACON` rows in a plain timestamped text format, with each floor's raster and GeoJSON shipped beside the traces under MIT-licensed tooling ([indoor-location-competition-20](https://github.com/location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20)). IPIN Track 3 uses the same idea with its open GetSensorData Android logger and four-letter-code text rows ([IPIN 2026 Track 3 annex](https://competition.ipin-conference.org/fileadmin/assets/Current_Competition/IPIN2026_Track3_TechnicalAnnex_v2.0.pdf)), and Fusion-DHL's three-mall dataset (16.2 km) took mall ground truth from "either sparse positions from real-time user clicks or dense trajectories from visual inertial SLAM" ([Fusion-DHL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08837)). Combining ARKit/ARCore pose output between QR scans every 20–40 m therefore gives sub-metre pseudo-ground-truth with nothing more than printed codes, and adopting the ILC 2.0 row conventions makes Atlas recordings replayable through public tooling. The recorder's JSON export should carry a metadata block modelled on Sensor Logger's — schema version, device, OS and app version, timezone, NTP offset — with epoch-nanosecond timestamps per row, the most widely copied practical schema ([awesome-sensor-logger](https://github.com/tszheichoi/awesome-sensor-logger)). Critically, every walk should also record `venue_id`, `floor_id`, a `map_version` hash of the floor GeoJSON, and the app version, so a replay can pin the exact map it was recorded against; no published convention for this exists, so Atlas must design it.

Public datasets are useful for testing fusion code but none is South African and licences vary. The two-mall Wi-Fi RSSI set from UPCT/Neuromobile (three floors/69 zones and two floors/124 zones) is **CC BY 4.0** ([Zenodo 3698238](https://zenodo.org/records/3698238)), as is UJIIndoorLoc ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/310/ujiindoorloc)); MLoc released IMU, BLE and geomagnetic traces from 37 malls and chose BLE plus magnetic over Wi-Fi precisely because half its customers used iPhones without a Wi-Fi RSSI API ([MLoc](https://www-users.cs.umn.edu/~fengqian/paper/mloc_mobicom22.pdf)). RoNIN and TLIO are non-commercial and must not feed a shippable model. Radio maps age — a 44-day study detected RSSI shifts with variance and Isolation Forest methods ([arXiv 2402.12756](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12756)) — but no validated refresh interval exists; Apple requires re-survey only when the AP set is upgraded or relocated ([Apple Indoor Maps Program](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf)), so an event-driven policy (AP changes, tenant fit-outs) with continuous drift monitoring beats a calendar. The most valuable research direction for a solo founder is crowdsourced self-calibration: Zee showed in 2012 that PDR plus floor-map matching lets ordinary users build a Wi-Fi radio map with the floor plan as the only site input ([Zee](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/zee-zero-effort-crowdsourcing-for-indoor-localization)), a 2025 semi-self-supervised approach learns from unlabeled trajectories and uses a much smaller labeled set to pseudo-label them ([arXiv 2504.03756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03756v1)), and a 2025 ISPRS paper corrects mall trajectories with inflection-point features and iteratively infers shop locations ([Ye & Chen](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-G-2025/1011/2025/)). A navigation session where the user visibly follows a route is a stronger prior than a floor plan, so Atlas's routed sessions can serve as weak labels for radio and magnetic maps once the checkpoint walks provide the small labeled seed — an inference the literature supports structurally but has not evaluated directly. For storage, MCAP is now the default rosbag2 backend and the right container for high-rate multi-stream logs, Parquet the right analytics format ([MCAP benchmarks](https://mcap.dev/guides/benchmarks/rosbag2-storage-plugins)); for versioning, lakeFS acquired DVC in November 2025 with both remaining open source, DVC fitting Git-tied file versioning and lakeFS branch/merge over object storage ([DEV comparison](https://dev.to/dibi8/dvc-vs-lakefs-vs-delta-lake-choosing-the-right-data-version-control-tool-for-ml-hf9)). At Atlas's scale, JSON plus DVC in the repo is enough; MCAP and lakeFS become worthwhile only when multiple venues produce hours of logs weekly.

The privacy posture is dictated by three regimes that happen to agree. POPIA lists "location information" explicitly as personal information, defines consent as a "voluntary, specific and informed expression of will", and defines de-identification to exclude anything re-identifiable by reasonably foreseeable linkage — so hashed MACs or persistent install IDs tied to trajectories are not de-identified ([popia.co.za s1](https://popia.co.za/section-1-definitions/)). Administrative fines reach R10 million, cross-border transfers under s72 require substantially similar protection or consent, and the Information Regulator has been fining actively since 2024 even though no location-specific action has yet appeared ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=ZA)). The GDPR precedent that should shape the analytics design is the Dutch DPA's **€600,000 fine of Enschede** for Wi-Fi footfall sensors: the regulator found no intent and no evidence that anyone was followed, yet ruled that monitoring which phone passes which sensor over time "turns counting into tracking people" and that deploying a system *capable* of this is itself a serious breach ([TechXplore/AP](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-04-dutch-city-fined-wi-fi-tracking.html)); Spain's AEPD now requires a DPIA before any Wi-Fi tracking deployment and rapid anonymisation ([AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/guides/wi-fi-tracking-technologies-guidance-for-data-controllers.pdf)). The store policies add prescriptive wording: Google Play treats BLE and Wi-Fi scanning that derives location as location use, requires a declaration form, demo video and an in-app disclosure containing the word "location" for any background or foreground-service-equivalent access ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)), and from **27 January 2027** apps targeting Android 17 must use the system location button for one-off requests and justify `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` ([Play policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241)); Apple requires purpose strings that "clearly and completely describe" data use under 5.1.1(ii) ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-purpose-strings)). Because the recorder is opt-in and foreground, Atlas can sidestep the background-location declaration entirely by never requesting it. The analytics endpoint should drop or rotate device identifiers before storage and publish only aggregated zone counts. For the warehouse, ClickHouse (Apache 2.0) fed by Atlas's own ingestion endpoint avoids Snowplow's January 2024 Limited Use License, which forbids running the collector "in production" ([Snowplow](https://snowplow.io/blog/introducing-snowplow-limited-use-license)); for aggregation, OpenDP's Rust-core library and the Tumult Analytics project it took over in October 2025 (used by the US Census Bureau) provide differentially private histograms ([OpenDP](https://opendp.org/2025/10/02/welcoming-tumult-analytics-and-tumult-core-to-opendp)). Google's own DP-heatmap work shows that error grows with grid resolution ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/differentially-private-heatmaps/)), which argues for shop-or-zone-level cells (the UPCT dataset's 69–124 zones per mall is the right granularity) rather than metre grids. This also matches what SA landlords already say publicly: Attacq's Mall of Africa analysis runs on aggregate device patterns, not individuals ([Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/property/2024-10-18-qa-attacq-enhances-mall-of-africa-experience-with-data-analytics/)).

## The 2026 market rewards owned stacks on Android-first phones

The commercial field has split into map-first platforms that sell per-square-metre subscriptions and treat positioning as an add-on, and positioning-first vendors that fingerprint or sell beacons, and almost none publishes prices. Mappedin — which runs Canal Walk's embedded web map and CMS ([Mappedin case study](https://www.mappedin.com/resources/case-study/canal-walk-shopping-centre-elevating-customer-engagement/)) — closed a **US$24.5 M Series B** announced in April 2026 and prices by square footage on request ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/mappedin-eyes-city-wide-indoor-mapping-with-24-5-million-series-b-round/); [Mappedin pricing](https://mappedin.com/pricing)). The only self-serve commercial positioning price found is IndoorAtlas Pro at **€349 per venue per month for up to 10,000 m²** — a super-regional SA mall would fall into unpublished enterprise tiers ([IndoorAtlas pricing](https://indooratlas.com/pricing)); Crowd Connected quotes US$8.5k–25k per venue per year with beacons ([Crowd Connected](https://www.crowdconnected.com/pricing)). The telling signal is who left: Pointr now sells into healthcare and events ([Pointr press](https://www.pointr.tech/press)), Kontakt.io raised $47.5 M to go into US hospitals ([Kontakt.io](https://kontakt.io/press-releases/kontakt-io-raises-47-5-million-series-c-funding-from-goldman-sachs-to-fuel-ai-development-and-expansion-into-us-hospitals/)), Phunware pivoted to hospitality on FY2025 revenue of $2.6 M ([Phunware](https://investors.phunware.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/346/phunware-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)), and MapsPeople, which bought Point Inside's 1,800 US mall maps, grew ARR only 12 % to DKK 59.8 M before being taken private and delisted in 2025 ([Cision](https://news.cision.com/mapspeople-a-s/r/mapspeople-a-s-announces-agreement-with-apex-group-technologies-backed-by-round13-capital-to-acquire,c4141284)). Mall wayfinding as a stand-alone SaaS has been a hard business; the survivors bundle maps, analytics and operator tooling. The platforms will not rescue a developer either: Apple's Indoor Maps FAQ says Apple Maps adds indoor maps "only for publicly accessible areas of airports", exposes only floor level through Core Location, and offers "no indoor wayfinding API" ([Apple FAQ](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program/faq)); Google's last indoor Live View expansion in February 2023 covered twelve cities with none in Africa ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/sustainable-immersive-maps-announcements/)). Atlas must own or license its positioning, and since Mappedin, IndoorAtlas, Situm and Pointr all ship React Native SDKs, owning it now does not foreclose licensing later.

South Africa's specifics sharpen the design. Flagship malls already have wayfinding but none with blue-dot positioning: Sandton City and Eastgate run ViaDirect kiosks with QR-launched mobile web maps ([ViaDirect](https://viadirect.com/digital-wayfinding-african-shopping-centres-sandton-city-eastgate/)), Canal Walk uses Mappedin Web, and MapIT reportedly mapped 65 local malls ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/?p=187718)). Secondary malls without digital wayfinding are the easier first targets, and exclusivity questions should be expected at the flagships. The device fleet is **81.8 % Android and 18.2 % iOS** (StatCounter, October 2026), with Samsung at roughly half of all devices ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/south-africa); [IntelPoint](https://intelpoint.co/insights/samsung-commands-over-half-of-south-africas-smartphone-market-at-51-5-as-of-july-2025/)) — so Android positioning quality on mid-tier Samsungs matters most, and iOS-only tricks miss four shoppers in five. Data is expensive at an average R20.50/GB and R85 for an ad hoc gigabyte at Vodacom and MTN ([Scrolla](https://scrolla.africa/sa-pays-more-for-data-than-27-other-african-countries-new-report-finds/); [TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/mtn-cuts-data-prices-to-match-vodacoms/170847/)), which makes the offline venue cache and a small PMTiles bundle a competitive requirement rather than polish. Landlords demonstrably buy analytics — Hyprop reports an average monthly foot count of 7.2 million across its portfolio and installed extra counters ([Hyprop IAR 2025](https://www.hyprop.co.za/pdf/investor/integrated-reports/2025/full-iar.pdf)), Attacq fuses Wi-Fi analytics with bank card-spend data, and MRI partnered with Pretoria's Fatti Location for footfall and dwell ([MRI](https://www.mrisoftware.com/za/news/mri-software-partners-with-fatti-location-to-deliver-advanced-retail-analytics-to-south-african-shopping-centres/)) — but they buy *passive* sensing, now shifting to CCTV AI ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/beyond-the-ping-why-cctv-analytics-is-replacing-legacy-wifi-for-sa-landlords/WnpNgM21R6l7VrGd)). Atlas's analytics only wins by adding what sensors cannot see: search intent and routed destinations. Sponsored search and route ads, by contrast, have weak evidence: Aisle411's sponsored pin drops (2012) and Point Inside's StoreBoost paid in-app search (2015) both ended in silence or asset sales ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/17/walgreens-gets-nationwide-in-store-navigation-but-not-in-its-own-app/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/retailers-can-now-sell-paid-search-in-their-apps-with-point-inside-storeboost-236493)), and only Google's own Promoted Pins are clearly alive, extended to Demand Gen in May 2025 ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-promoted-pins-maps-demand-gen-456126)). Both historical ad businesses relied on product-level search in grocery and big-box stores; mall store search has far lower query volume per venue, so treat sponsored search as a hypothesis to test cheaply, not a plan to build around.

On the React Native side the constraints are now fixed and mostly favourable. Expo SDK 55+ cannot disable the New Architecture ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/new-architecture)), so every library must be Fabric/TurboModule/JSI/Nitro-capable and nearly all need a development build. For IMU ingestion, expo-sensors `DeviceMotion` at a 10 ms interval is adequate — iOS hardware delivers at least 100 Hz, Android caps at 200 Hz without `HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS` ([Expo DeviceMotion](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/devicemotion.md); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager/devicemotionupdateinterval.md)) — but it exposes a single fused rotation with no selectable reference frame, so a small Expo Modules API module in Kotlin/Swift is the right way to obtain a magnetometer-free game-rotation-vector attitude, true sensor timestamps and batched typed arrays ([Expo Modules API](https://docs.expo.dev/modules/overview.md)). For BLE, upstream `react-native-ble-plx` documents compatibility only to RN 0.74/Expo 51 ([ble-plx](https://github.com/dotintent/react-native-ble-plx)); the realistic choices are its maintained TurboModule fork `@sfourdrinier/react-native-ble-plx` (RN 0.86+/Expo 57+) ([FORK.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@sfourdrinier/react-native-ble-plx@3.9.2/docs/FORK.md)) or the Nitro-based `react-native-ble-nitro` (MIT), whose scan results carry manufacturer data so iBeacon and Eddystone frames can be parsed in JS without a beacon library ([ble-nitro](https://github.com/zykeco/react-native-ble-nitro)). Two traps matter: never set `neverForLocation`, because it filters iBeacons from scan results and asserts the app derives no location ([Android Bluetooth permissions](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/bluetooth/bt-permissions)); and keep one continuous scan running, since Android allows only five scan start/stops per 30 s ([Punch Through](https://punchthrough.com/android-ble-scan-errors/)). No RN library exposes `WifiRttManager`, so RTT is a ~200-line Kotlin Expo Module ([WifiRttManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/rtt/WifiRttManager)). For the engine itself, `react-native-worklets`' `createWorkletRuntime` plus `runOnRuntime` provides a dedicated background JS thread with an event loop and mutex-locked JSI ([worklets docs](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-worklets/docs/threading/createWorkletRuntime)); every commercial RN indoor SDK runs its engine natively, but a 100 Hz PDR plus a few-thousand-particle filter should fit a Hermes worklet runtime, and the native rewrite should wait for profiling evidence. If a learned model is added later, `react-native-fast-tflite` (MIT, Nitro, zero-copy ArrayBuffers, `runSync`) is the simplest fit for a small 1-D CNN ([fast-tflite](https://github.com/mrousavy/react-native-fast-tflite)), while `react-native-executorch` requires RN 0.83+/Expo 55+ and a pinned `react-native-worklets` range that must be checked against Reanimated 4 ([executorch](https://github.com/software-mansion/react-native-executorch)). Screen-off navigation is the one feature to defer: the only Expo-supported keep-alive is `expo-location`'s foreground service and iOS `location` background mode ([Expo Location](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/location.md)), iOS Core Motion stops within seconds of backgrounding unless a Core Location session runs ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/841001)), continuous BLE scanning costs 94–154 mA on a measured handset ([arXiv 2102.03351](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03351)), and both stores subject it to background-location review. Foreground navigation with `keepAwake` avoids all of it.

## Prioritised recommendations for a solo founder on a limited budget

The ordering principle is simple: do first the things that need no hardware, no partner and no money, that de-risk the next step, and that produce evidence a mall operator or investor can see. Every item below uses MIT/Apache/BSD components or platform APIs; the only cash items are printed QR codes, a handful of beacons in phase two, and object storage.

| Priority | Horizon | Action | Why now | Key components |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Weeks 1–4 | Replace synthetic positioning with a real particle filter on the corridor graph, with QR checkpoints as absolute fixes and barometer transitions gated to vertical connectors | Zero hardware, uses existing graph and recorder; this is the competition-winning architecture | TypeScript on a `createWorkletRuntime` thread; IPIN Q3 + floor-penalty metric in the replay tool |
| 2 | Weeks 2–6 | Formalise the recorder: ILC 2.0-style `WAYPOINT` rows, Sensor Logger-style metadata, `map_version` hash per walk; do 20–30 checkpoint walks in the pilot venue | Turns the opt-in recorder into a labelled dataset that every later step depends on | JSON + DVC in-repo; MCAP/lakeFS only when volumes justify |
| 3 | Weeks 4–8 | Ship a native Expo Module for batched IMU with game-rotation-vector attitude; add ARKit/ARCore pose streaming as an optional motion model | Removes magnetic-disturbance heading errors in a steel-and-glass mall; VIO drift is an order of magnitude lower than step-PDR | Expo Modules API (Kotlin/Swift) |
| 4 | Months 2–3 | Build the dashboard editor: MapLibre GL JS + Terra Draw over a georeferenced plan; auto-generate edges with `CG_ApproximateMedialAxis` → pgRouting; add an IMDF import/export adapter | Ends hand tracing, makes the second venue cheap, and makes Atlas data interoperable with Apple/Mappedin ecosystems | Next.js, Terra Draw (MIT), PostGIS 3.5 SFCGAL, pgRouting, `straight-skeleton` WASM for preview |
| 5 | Months 2–4 | Migrate the phone renderer to `@maplibre/maplibre-react-native` v11 with per-venue PMTiles in the offline cache | Needed before venue two; offline tiles answer the R85/GB data-cost objection | Martin for live tiles, tippecanoe for PMTiles, `level` filters |
| 6 | Months 3–5 | Add BLE as a cross-platform fix with a sparse pilot (7–10 beacons per 1,000 m² of corridor), Android Wi-Fi RSSI as a slow corrector behind a capability check, and magnetic edge profiles collected during checkpoint walks | Cuts drift resets from "every QR scan" to continuous; still no survey campaign | `react-native-ble-nitro` or the `@sfourdrinier` fork; FIND3/Anyplace as backend reference |
| 7 | Months 4–6 | Privacy and analytics foundation: purpose-specific POPIA consent copy, identifier rotation at ingestion, ClickHouse warehouse, DP zone counts via OpenDP; write the DPIA | Required before any landlord analytics conversation; Enschede shows design, not intent, is judged | ClickHouse (Apache 2.0), OpenDP/Tumult |
| 8 | Months 6–12 | Crowdsourced self-calibration: use routed navigation sessions as weak labels to grow radio/magnetic maps from the checkpoint seed | Converts users into surveyors; the only path to multi-venue coverage without site visits | Zee-style map matching; semi-self-supervised pseudo-labelling |
| Defer | 2027+ | Screen-off navigation, Wi-Fi RTT module, UWB/Channel Sounding, learned inertial models, VPS | Store review burden, hardware dependence or licence problems outweigh benefit today | Revisit when Android 16 `RangingManager` and BT 6.3 anchors are common |

Three decisions deserve explicit justification. First, build the engine in TypeScript on a worklet runtime rather than natively: the commercial SDKs run native engines, but a solo founder's bottleneck is iteration speed, and the replay tool already exists in the TypeScript world; move to Kotlin/Swift only when profiling shows a saturated core. Second, keep the venue graph proprietary while adopting IMDF for interchange and SIT vocabulary for tile layers: no standard carries a routing network, so the graph is the moat, while interoperability lowers the cost of every partner conversation. Third, do not request background location, do not set `neverForLocation`, and do not store device-linked trajectories beyond the consented recorder: each of these choices removes a Play/App Store review hurdle or a POPIA exposure at essentially no product cost, and the Android 17 location-button rule arriving in January 2027 will reward apps that already request the minimum. On go-to-market, target a secondary mall without digital wayfinding for the pilot, offer the operator a written source-plan licence in exchange for exclusivity-free data rights, and sell the analytics as intent data (searches, routed destinations, aggregated zone dwell) rather than footfall, which landlords already buy from sensors and banks.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Atlas's problem. The founder's instinct that positioning is the hard part is correct, but the hard part is not the algorithm — map-matched PDR with particle filtering is settled science with a known 3–5 m ceiling for hardware-free deployments — it is the *data loop*: labelled walks, a versioned map that recordings pin to, and a path from a few surveyor walks to crowdsourced self-calibration. Every open-source project worth adopting here (MapLibre RN, Martin, tippecanoe, Terra Draw, PostGIS SFCGAL, pgRouting, ClickHouse, OpenDP) is permissively licensed and actively maintained in 2026, while every project that promises a shortcut on the algorithm itself (RoNIN, TLIO, hloc, ORB-SLAM3, CubiCasa5k) is GPL, non-commercial or research-grade. The cheap path and the legally safe path coincide.

The deeper implication is strategic. Vendors who sold mall wayfinding alone have pivoted or stalled; those who survived bundle maps, operator tooling and analytics, and South African landlords already pay for passive footfall. Atlas's durable advantages are therefore not blue-dot accuracy per se but an owned, auto-generated routing graph over IMDF-compatible venue data, an engine tuned for the mid-tier Android phones that four in five South African shoppers carry, offline-first delivery that respects R85-a-gigabyte data, and a privacy architecture that can show a landlord aggregate intent without ever being able to follow a single phone. Build those four things in that order and the positioning accuracy will follow from the data they generate.

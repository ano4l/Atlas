## Key Question 1: Cheap ground-truthing methods for walks (checkpoints, QR/NFC, ARCore/ARKit VIO, LiDAR phones, surveyor wheels)

### Takeaway
The dominant low-cost approach used by the main benchmarks (Microsoft Indoor Location Competition 2.0, IPIN Track 3, NIST PerfLoc, Fusion-DHL) is a human walker tapping pre-surveyed waypoints/landmarks on a floor-plan at the moment they pass them; phone VIO (ARKit/ARCore) gives dense but drifting pseudo-ground-truth (order 0.02 m/s drift for ARKit in one benchmark) that is best anchored by sparse checkpoints. Phone LiDAR is adequate for floor-plan-grade mapping (roughly 8–20 cm) but not survey-grade.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Indoor Location Competition 2.0 ground truth: "the site-surveyor labels each location on the map"; walker holds an Android phone flat in front of the body while a recording app logs sensor data; waypoints are `TYPE_WAYPOINT` rows with x, y in metres on the floor-plan — [location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20 README](https://github.com/location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20)
- Each Microsoft path trace file covers "an indoor path between two positions, walked by a site surveyor"; floor-plan metadata (raster image, size, GeoJSON) is included per floor — [Microsoft Research dataset page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=766744)
- NIST PerfLoc training data includes "timestamps at marked test points in buildings, with known coordinates" and records when a walker changes direction or mobility mode; test sets have no ground truth — [NIST PerfLoc](https://www.nist.gov/node/1833376) (via search snippet)
- Fusion-DHL (WiFi + IMU + floorplan, Sachini Herath et al.): dataset covers 9.6 h (26.1 km) over 4 university buildings and 5.7 h (16.2 km) over 3 shopping malls; mall ground truth comes from "either sparse positions from real-time user clicks or dense trajectories from visual inertial SLAM"; IMU at 200 Hz; code/data/models promised public at github.com/Sachini/Fusion-DHL — [arXiv 2105.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08837) (snippet) and [abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08837)
- IPIN Track 3 (smartphone, offsite): logfiles are plain text, one row per sensor reading, each row starting with a 4-letter sensor code and semicolon (e.g. `WIFI;`, `ACC;`, `MAGN;`), fields include app timestamp, sensor timestamp, readings and accuracy; WiFi RSS in dBm; 2024 annex says "same data format as in the previous edition"; 2025/2026 annexes say the GetSensorsData app was updated for recent smartphones and that weak BLE signals were not recorded; scoring trials served via the EvAAL API — [IPIN 2026 Track 3 Technical Annex v2.0](https://competition.ipin-conference.org/fileadmin/assets/Current_Competition/IPIN2026_Track3_TechnicalAnnex_v2.0.pdf), [IPIN 2016 Track 3 annex](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/fileadmin/assets/Previous_competition/2016/Competition/track3_ipin2016competition.pdf), [IPIN 2024 Track 3 annex](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/fileadmin/2024/IPIN2024_Track03_TechnicalAnnex_V01.pdf) (all via search snippets; PDFs not parseable by fetch tool)
- Benchmark of four off-the-shelf VIO systems (ARKit, ARCore, RealSense T265, ZED 2) against OptiTrack: ARKit "showed the most stable and high accuracy/consistency" with a relative pose drift error of about 0.02 m per second — [arXiv 2207.06780](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.06780)
- ADVIO dataset (iPhone): 6-DoF ground truth "computed by combining a recent pure inertial navigation system (INS) with frequent manually determined position fixes based on a precise floor plan"; compares Tango, ARCore and ARKit tracks; ARCore poses at 30 Hz — [ADVIO arXiv 1807.09828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09828v1.pdf); repo [AaltoVision/ADVIO](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2FAaltoVision%2FADVIO)
- MARViN dataset: camera images, ARCore/ARKit VIO data, raw sensor data and ground-truth poses; authors report ARKit/ARCore "closely approximate ground truth within a short timeframe" — [MARViN](https://lck666666.github.io/research/MARViN)
- IDOL dataset (CMU): ground truth from a LiDAR-visual-inertial SLAM rig; iPhone and rig time-aligned using a physical "sync spike" rather than NTP — [arXiv 2309.17187](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17187v2)
- iPhone 12 Pro LiDAR scans: using a gimbal "significantly improves" accuracy; roughly 15–20 cm RMSE with gimbal vs ~1.4 cm for photogrammetry; authors conclude not suitable for high-precision engineering but fine for medium-accuracy mapping — [Ferdowsi Univ. civil eng. paper](https://civil-ferdowsi.um.ac.ir/article_45986_3f76394ff8a9398b3bf2268fb9ae38aa.pdf)
- iPhone 12 Pro Max LiDAR 2D floor plan vs total station: RMSE ±0.08 m on side lengths, ±0.28 m² on areas — [DergiPark MEPHOJ 2024](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/mephoj/issue/80446/1354998)
- iPad Pro LiDAR (Teo & Yang 2023): recommends 1–1.5 m sensor-to-target distance; a comparable-app study reported ±3 cm horizontal, ±7 mm vertical absolute accuracy — [Developments in the Built Environment 2023](https://doaj.org/article/9cbab8e9cd04423aae61f174ca458b1e)
- Polycam's own guide: capture technique was "the single largest variable", larger than hardware — [Polycam accuracy article](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/50434745985556-How-Accurate-Are-Polycam-Scans)
- Apple Indoor Maps Program: venues build an IMDF map, then run an RF survey with Apple's Indoor Survey app; the app has a "Positioning" mode for testing accuracy; re-survey required if the AP set is upgraded or relocated — [Apple Indoor Maps Program PDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf), [Mappedin IMDF guide](https://www.mappedin.com/blog/product/map-editor/product-101-imdf-export/)

### Inferences
- Your existing QR-code checkpoints are functionally equivalent to the "waypoint click" protocol used by Microsoft ILC 2.0, IPIN Track 3 and NIST PerfLoc; adopting their file conventions (one timestamped `WAYPOINT` row per checkpoint, in floor-plan metres, plus a per-floor GeoJSON/raster) would make your recordings directly comparable with public tooling.
- Combining ARKit/ARCore pose output (dense, ~2 cm/s drift) with QR checkpoints every 20–40 m would give sub-metre pseudo-ground-truth between checkpoints; this mirrors Fusion-DHL's "sparse clicks or dense VI-SLAM" design.
- A sync event (e.g., a tap/spike at a QR scan) is a cheap way to align phone IMU and an external reference; IDOL used exactly this.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying the positional accuracy of walker-tapped waypoints themselves (human reaction latency × walking speed); IPIN annex PDFs could not be parsed to extract their landmark procedure or 75th-percentile error metric details.
- No dataset found that used a surveyor's measuring wheel as ground truth.
- No study found on NFC tags as ground-truth markers.

## Key Question 2: Radio survey / fingerprint collection apps, file formats, and refresh frequency in malls

### Takeaway
Open logger apps worth copying from are GetSensorData (IPIN's reference Android logger, SoftwareX 2022), Sensor Logger (closed-source app, MIT-licensed docs/tooling, ns-epoch CSV/JSON/SQLite exports, Wi-Fi only on Android), FIND3 (MIT, Go, Wi-Fi/BLE fingerprinting) and Anyplace (MIT, Scala/Kotlin). The dominant interchange formats are simple timestamped text/CSV rows (IPIN, Microsoft ILC 2.0) or HDF5/NumPy (RoNIN, TLIO). No validated rule-of-thumb refresh interval exists; studies show measurable RSSI drift over 44 days and 25 months, and Apple requires re-survey only when APs are upgraded or relocated.

### Cited Findings
**Survey/logger apps**
- GetSensorData: "open-source, flexible, and extensible framework for registering sensor data from Android devices", SoftwareX July 2022 (Gutiérrez, Jiménez, Seco, Álvarez, Aguilera, Torres-Sospedra, Melchor) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4391def7c3c84e34b249b2be73ae9a57); a repo `lopsi/GetSensorData_Android` (~50 stars, last updated ~5 years ago per aggregator) exists but could not be opened to confirm license/current version — [relatedrepos listing](https://relatedrepos.com/gh/lopsi/GetSensorData_Android)
- IPIN 2025/2026 Track 3 uses "GetSensorsData" and the annex says "We have updated the GetSensorsData APP to be compatible with recent smartphones" — [IPIN 2025 Track 3 annex](https://competition.ipin-conference.org/fileadmin/2025/IPIN2025_-_track_3_technical_annex_v2.pdf) (snippet)
- Sensor Logger (iOS/Android/watch): records accelerometer (raw+calibrated), gravity, gyroscope, orientation, magnetometer, compass, GPS, barometric altitude/pressure, pedometer, BLE advertisements (raw manufacturer data), and "Nearby Wi-Fi networks are logged on Android only"; exports ZIP/CSV/JSON/KML/SQLite (combined resampled CSV is paid); CSV has one file per sensor with `time` (UNIX epoch nanoseconds UTC) and `seconds_elapsed`; `Metadata.csv` has schema version, device, platform, OS/app version, timezone; optional NTP sync to time.google.com (median of 8 requests, refresh every 3 h, "tens of milliseconds" precision); HTTP and MQTT live streaming; docs repo `tszheichoi/awesome-sensor-logger` is MIT, 392 stars, 172 commits — [awesome-sensor-logger](https://github.com/tszheichoi/awesome-sensor-logger); IPIN 2025 short paper by the developer — [CEUR-WS Vol-4047 short5](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4047/short5.pdf)
- FIND3 (`schollz/find3`): MIT, Go server + React client, ~4.8k stars, 604 commits, not archived; components: data storage server, ML server, CLI scanner, Android scanner (`find3-android-scanner`), ESP8266/ESP32 client; "Any data source - Bluetooth / WiFi / magnetic fields / etc." — [github.com/schollz/find3](https://github.com/schollz/find3). No last-commit date visible; maintenance status unverified.
- Anyplace (`dmsl/anyplace`, Univ. of Cyprus): MIT, server in Scala (Play Framework) + MongoDB, core/Android libs in Kotlin, 752 stars, 1,086 commits, server 4.3.1 released as part of Anyplace 5.0 "early 2022"; web apps Architect/Viewer, Android clients, Swagger API; no releases page entries; no accuracy figures beyond tagline — [github.com/dmsl/anyplace](https://github.com/dmsl/anyplace). Logger app not confirmed on the page.
- OpenHPS (`OpenHPS/openhps-core`): Apache-2.0, TypeScript 5.9+, 35 stars, 1,770 commits, copyright 2019–2026, "Maintained by MVdW-Software" (VUB); JOSS 2025 paper describes it as a modular graph-based framework — [openhps-core](https://github.com/OpenHPS/openhps-core), [OpenHPS docs](https://openhps.org/docs/introduction/), [VUB publication](https://researchportal.vub.be/en/publications/openhps-a-modular-framework-to-facilitate-the-development-of-fair/)
- Apple Indoor Survey app: collects Wi-Fi data for upload to Apple; positioning relies on 2.4 GHz radios ("beaconing mode" recommended if the venue runs 5 GHz only); no extra hardware — [Apple Indoor Maps Program PDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf); app first appeared 2015, reportedly linked to Apple's WiFiSLAM acquisition — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/news/apple-indoor-positioning-technology/)

**File formats**
- Microsoft ILC 2.0 trace format: `.txt`, columns = Unix ms timestamp, data type, values; ten types incl. `TYPE_WAYPOINT`, `TYPE_WIFI` (SSID, BSSID, RSSI, frequency, last-seen ts), `TYPE_BEACON` (UUID, major, minor, txPower, RSSI, distance, MAC, ts); repo MIT, Python sample code, 201 stars — [indoor-location-competition-20](https://github.com/location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20)
- IPIN logfile: text rows prefixed by 4-letter sensor code + semicolon-separated fields (see KQ1) — [IPIN 2016 annex](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/fileadmin/assets/Previous_competition/2016/Competition/track3_ipin2016competition.pdf) (snippet)
- RoNIN (`Sachini/ronin`): GPL-3.0, Python, 432 stars, 5 commits; collected with "an App for Google Tango Device" and "an App for any Android Device"; "due to security concerns we were unable to publish 50% of our dataset"; data license is custom, non-commercial research only — [github.com/Sachini/ronin](https://github.com/Sachini/ronin), [FRDR record](https://www.frdr-dfdr.ca/repo/dataset/816d1e8c-1fc3-47ff-b8ea-a36ff51d682a); community write-up reports HDF5 groups `raw`, `synced`, `pose`, `tango` — [armanasq RoNIN notes](https://armanasq.github.io/datsets/ronin-datset) (unofficial)
- TLIO (`CathIAS/TLIO`, Meta): NumPy-based per-sequence folders (`calibration.json`, `imu0_resampled.npy`, `imu0_resampled_description.json`, `imu_samples_0.csv`), train/val/test list files; archive filename includes "cc-by-nc"; 414 stars, 91 commits; LICENSE file present but text not shown (aggregator lists "other") — [github.com/CathIAS/TLIO](https://github.com/CathIAS/TLIO)
- Navigine publishes its own navigation log format — [Navigine docs](https://docs.navigine.com/navigation_log_format)

**Refresh frequency / radio-map aging**
- Apple: "Adding or removing a single access point has little effect, but if the set of Wi-Fi access points is upgraded or relocated in a building or facility then a re-survey of the building using Apple's Indoor Survey app will be required" — [Apple Indoor Maps Program PDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf)
- 44-day RSSI tracking study found temporal shifts in Wi-Fi RSSI using variance and Isolation Forest, comparing static vs dynamic databases — [arXiv 2402.12756](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12756)
- 25-month study of signal-strength variation for adaptive long-term Wi-Fi localisation — [MDPI Sensors 24(17):5665](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/17/5665)
- IPIN 2023 paper on overcoming radio-map degradation: removes disappeared and mobile APs before enriching with newly detected APs — [AMiner record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/6571a08f939a5f4082376a64/overcoming-radio-map-degradation-in-wi-fi-based-positioning-systems)
- IEEE Access 2019: incremental online update combining historical RSS, new RSS and purposeful probe data to cut samples needed for frequent updates — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/573f991fdd07445f9a888eb1ccb11c19)
- 2025 survey of AI-driven positioning groups fingerprint-update methods into GAN-synthesised time-aware maps, incremental learning and transfer learning — [arXiv 2501.14970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14970)
- MLoc (MobiCom 2022, 37 malls): used BLE + geomagnetic rather than Wi-Fi because "around half of their customers use iPhone, which does not offer public APIs for querying WiFi APs' RSSI" — [MLoc paper](https://www-users.cs.umn.edu/~fengqian/paper/mloc_mobicom22.pdf)

### Inferences
- Since iOS exposes no Wi-Fi RSSI API, a mall fingerprint pipeline must treat Wi-Fi as Android-only and rely on BLE + magnetic + IMU for cross-platform coverage (MLoc's conclusion; Sensor Logger's Android-only Wi-Fi logging confirms the constraint).
- Your JSON walk export should carry a `Metadata` block modelled on Sensor Logger's (schema version, device, OS, app version, timezone, epoch start, NTP offset) and per-row epoch-ns timestamps; this is the most widely copied practical schema.
- Absent a validated interval, a defensible policy is event-driven re-survey (AP changes, tenant fit-outs) plus continuous crowdsourced drift monitoring (per-AP RSSI variance / Isolation Forest as in arXiv 2402.12756), rather than a fixed calendar cadence.

### Gaps
- No published mall-specific figure for how fast fingerprints decay or an industry-recommended re-survey interval.
- Could not verify current license/maintenance of the GetSensorData GitHub repository or whether IPIN's updated 2025/2026 app is published.
- OxIOD license and file format not found (project page http://deepio.cs.ox.ac.uk; 158 sequences, >42 km) — [OxIOD](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/paper/oxiod-the-dataset-for-deep-inertial-odometry)

## Key Question 3: Crowdsourcing and self-calibration (SLAM-style radio-map building, learned approaches 2023–2026, route-following users as weak labels)

### Takeaway
The canonical designs are Zee (MobiCom 2012, PDR + floor-map matching, only input is a floor map) and UnLoc (PDR + organically detected sensor "anchors"); Apple's indoor positioning descends from its WiFiSLAM acquisition. Recent work (2023–2026) replaces hand-tuned map matching with learned trajectory correction and semi-/self-supervised representation learning over unlabeled crowdsourced trajectories, using a small labeled set for pseudo-labels, and uses landmarks/inflection points and shop-location inference in malls.

### Cited Findings
- Zee (Rai et al., MobiCom 2012): goal is to make WiFi fingerprint calibration effortless; uses phone inertial sensors to track users while running WiFi scans; "the only site-specific input it needs is a floor map"; handles unknown start location, stride length and phone placement — [Microsoft Research Zee page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/zee-zero-effort-crowdsourcing-for-indoor-localization), [Zee PDF](https://modernmobile.cs.washington.edu/docs/zee.pdf)
- Zee vs UnLoc: both dead-reckon and attach WiFi fingerprints; Zee corrects drift by map matching against the floorplan, UnLoc by "distinctive anchors in the environment detected from multi-sensor signatures" — [arXiv 2106.13663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.13663)
- A 2026 arXiv survey tabulates Zee and UnLoc among IMU+WiFi crowdsourcing systems — [arXiv 2605.11038](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11038v1); 2025 arXiv paper "Crowdsourcing Ubiquitous Indoor Localization with…" — [arXiv 2506.18317](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18317v2)
- Crowd-sensing SLAM with probabilistic similarity models fuses PDR with RSS from surrounding APs to estimate trajectories and map the radio environment simultaneously — [arXiv 1904.11712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.11712)
- Leto (IEEE TMC 2024): builds a radio map from crowdsourced WiFi + accelerometer plus a few landmarks, learning topology from combined user traces — [Leto PDF](https://home.cse.ust.hk/~gchan/papers/TMC24_leto.pdf)
- IEEE TMC paper accepted Sept 2025 (Tianjin Univ.): notes landmark/floor-plan methods "work well on noisy crowd data" but cannot be used in buildings lacking true location references — [TJU record](https://cam.tju.edu.cn/en/research/downAchiev.php?id=928)
- Ye & Chen, ISPRS Annals 2025, "Crowdsourced Indoor Positioning: Integrating 5G NR and WiFi": trajectories corrected using inflection-point features to build a WiFi–5G fingerprint database; iteratively infers shop locations to build a semantic map in a shopping mall — [ISPRS Annals X-G-2025](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-G-2025/1011/2025/)
- "Semi-Self Representation Learning for Crowdsourced WiFi Trajectories" (Kuo, Tseng, Chiang, Chen, April 2025): learns from unlabeled crowdsourced WiFi trajectories, then uses a much smaller labeled set to assign pseudo-labels (|C| ≫ |C̃|) — [arXiv 2504.03756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03756v1)
- "Multimodal Indoor Localization Using Crowdsourced Radio Maps" (Yi, Wen, Xia, Li, Zampella et al., rev. March 2024): fuses IMU with WiFi and uses crowdsourced radio maps in place of floor plans — [arXiv 2311.10601](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.10601v2)
- "WiFi Structure-from-Motion" (ICRA 2022): uses WiFi + IMU from everyday use and a self-supervised network to extract motion constraints — [ICRA 2022 PDF](https://mpil.sookmyung.ac.kr/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/2022_ICRA_WiFi_official.pdf)
- Geomagnetism-aided crowdsourced Wi-Fi radio-map construction (Sensors 2018) — [PMC5982238](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5982238/); robust crowdsourcing-based localisation — [PMC5424741](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5424741/)
- Apple: indoor positioning "achieves GPS-level accuracy in indoor spaces by using the radio frequency (RF) patterns of your Wi-Fi access points" from a venue survey (not crowdsourced) — [Apple Indoor Maps Program PDF](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/Apple-Indoor-Maps-Program.pdf); Apple acquired WiFiSLAM (reported) — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/news/apple-indoor-positioning-technology/)
- Fusion-DHL used "sparse positions from real-time user clicks" as one ground-truth mode in malls — [arXiv 2105.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08837)

### Inferences
- "Route-following users as weak labels" is not a named technique in the literature found, but it is structurally identical to Zee's map-matching prior (the route is a stronger prior than the floor plan) and to the 2025 semi-self-supervised approach (a small labeled set — your QR-checkpoint walks — pseudo-labels the large unlabeled set of navigation sessions). Inflection points in a followed route (turns at corridor junctions) are the "landmarks" exploited by Ye & Chen 2025 and Leto.
- Shop-location inference from dwell patterns (Ye & Chen 2025) is directly applicable to mall semantic maps and could double as a venue-analytics feature.

### Gaps
- No paper found that explicitly evaluates navigation-route adherence as a weak-supervision signal; the inference above is unverified.
- Google's current crowdsourced indoor approach is undocumented publicly in the sources found.
- Original WiFi-SLAM (Ferris et al.) paper not surfaced in searches; cite from the survey if needed.

## Key Question 4: Public datasets for development/benchmarking (licenses, formats)

### Takeaway
For mall-like development the most relevant open sets are Microsoft ILC 2.0 (hundreds of Chinese buildings, Wi-Fi/BLE/mag/IMU + waypoints, text traces, MIT code; dataset license not stated), the UPCT/Neuromobile two-mall Wi-Fi RSSI set (CC BY 4.0), Fusion-DHL (3 malls, 200 Hz IMU), and MLoc (37 malls, BLE/mag/IMU, 43 GB). Inertial-odometry sets (RoNIN, TLIO) are non-commercial; UJIIndoorLoc is CC BY 4.0. No 2025–2026 mall dataset combining Wi-Fi, BLE and IMU was found.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Indoor Location Competition 2.0 (2021): "dense indoor signatures of WiFi, geomagnetic field, iBeacons etc., as well as ground truth (waypoint) locations collected from hundreds of buildings in Chinese cities"; Kaggle competition "Indoor Location & Navigation" (kaggle.com/c/indoor-location-navigation) had ~1,446 contestants from >60 countries in 1,170 teams; dataset page states no license — [Microsoft Research dataset page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=766744), [competition page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=199902); code repo MIT — [GitHub](https://github.com/location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20); TIB DOI 10.57702/6uj2c5u4 — [TIB record](https://service.tib.eu/ldmservice/dataset/microsoft-indoor-location-competition-2-0)
- Wi-Fi RSSI fingerprint dataset from two malls (Lopez Pastor, Ruiz Ruiz, García Sánchez, Gómez Tornero; Neuromobile/UPCT), published 2020-03-16, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.3698238, CC BY 4.0, 278.2 MB CSV (`building.csv`, `floor.csv`, `zone.csv`, `training.csv`, `route.csv`, `euclidean_distance.csv`…); Mall 1: 3 floors/69 zones; Mall 2: 2 floors/124 zones; training via random-walk survey with 4 (page elsewhere says 5) smartphones; validation routes with 10 smartphones; samples = RSSI dBm, timestamp, channel, AP MAC — [Zenodo 3698238](https://zenodo.org/records/3698238)
- Fusion-DHL: 3 shopping malls (5.7 h / 16.2 km) + 4 university buildings (9.6 h / 26.1 km); IMU 200 Hz; WiFi positions from Google Fused Location Provider — [arXiv 2105.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08837)
- MLoc (MobiCom 2022): released dataset with IMU, BLE, geomagnetic and ground truth from 37 shopping malls, 43 GB at publication — [MLoc paper](https://www-users.cs.umn.edu/~fengqian/paper/mloc_mobicom22.pdf)
- UJIIndoorLoc: UCI ML Repository, CC BY 4.0, 21,048 instances, WLAN fingerprinting — [UCI dataset 310](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/310/ujiindoorloc)
- RoNIN: 42.7 h IMU data, 276 sequences, 3 buildings ("2.9 times more than OxIOD"); custom non-commercial research license (LICENSE.txt); 50% withheld — [RoNIN paper](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.12853), [FRDR record](https://www.frdr-dfdr.ca/repo/dataset/816d1e8c-1fc3-47ff-b8ea-a36ff51d682a), [GitHub](https://github.com/Sachini/ronin)

- OxIOD: 158 sequences, >42 km, http://deepio.cs.ox.ac.uk; license/format not found — [OxIOD](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/paper/oxiod-the-dataset-for-deep-inertial-odometry)
- Hilti SLAM Challenge dataset: indoor offices/labs/construction + outdoor sites; hosted at hilti-challenge.com; TIB DOI 10.57702/hd1bh1ta; license not stated in results — [arXiv 2109.11316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11316v3), [TIB record](https://service.tib.eu/ldmservice/dataset/bibtex/the-hilti-slam-challenge-dataset)
- IPIN competition data: 2025 Track 3 training data and "Trial44fixed" downloadable as zips, testing trials via EvaalAPI; Track 4 foot-mounted IMU data — [EvAAL 2025 data](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/2025/data); 2022 data — [EvAAL 2022 data](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/2022/data)
- Tampere Wi-Fi fingerprint dataset on Zenodo distributed under MIT — [Zenodo 1226835](https://zenodo.org/records/1226835)
- ADVIO (iPhone, indoor/outdoor, Tango/ARCore/ARKit tracks) — [arXiv 1807.09828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09828v1.pdf); MARViN — [MARViN](https://lck666666.github.io/research/MARViN); NIST PerfLoc — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/node/1833376)

### Inferences
- For a commercial product, only CC BY 4.0 sets (UJIIndoorLoc, two-mall Zenodo set) and MIT-licensed code are clearly safe; RoNIN/TLIO can train research prototypes but not shippable models without renegotiation. Microsoft ILC 2.0 usage terms must be checked on Kaggle before commercial use.
- Microsoft ILC 2.0 is the closest public analogue to your target (multi-floor commercial buildings, Wi-Fi+BLE+IMU, waypoint ground truth) and its trace format is a good target for your replay tooling.

- Hilti and OxIOD licenses unverified.

## Key Question 5: Storage and versioning (Parquet, Zarr, MCAP, ROS bags; DVC, lakeFS, Hugging Face; linking logs to map versions)

### Takeaway
MCAP is now the default rosbag2 storage backend and the preferred container for high-rate multi-stream sensor logs; Parquet is the downstream analytics format. For versioning, lakeFS acquired DVC in November 2025 (both remain open source): DVC fits Git-tied, file-level dataset/model versioning, lakeFS fits branch/commit/merge over object-storage data lakes. Hugging Face replaced Git LFS with Xet (chunk-level dedup) during 2025. No source specifically addresses linking sensor logs to map versions; this must be designed (see inferences).

### Cited Findings
- MCAP "is the default storage backend for rosbag2 since the ROS 2 Iron release"; ROS 1 rosbag tied to ROS 1, which reached end of life in 2025 — [Segments.ai MCAP vs ROS bag](https://segments.ai/?p=7371), [MCAP rosbag2 plugin benchmarks](https://mcap.dev/guides/benchmarks/rosbag2-storage-plugins)
- Since ROS 2 Humble MCAP "has become the recommended storage backend due to better random access performance and smaller file sizes" (vendor-affiliated claim) — [Trossen Robotics formats comparison](https://www.trossenrobotics.com/post/robotics-dataset-formats-comparison)
- Foxglove benchmark: `rosbag info` does "a dramatically larger number of disk seeks and reads than `mcap info`" (vendor benchmark) — [Foxglove blog](https://foxglove.dev/blog/mcap-vs-ros1-bag-index-performance)
- Parquet "is better suited for structured data lakes where query speed … is the main goal"; MCAP "better for high-frequency sensor streams where seek performance matters more than query flexibility" — [claru.ai MCAP guide](https://claru.ai/formats/mcap), [claru.ai ROS bag guide](https://claru.ai/formats/ros-bag)
- Original MCAP announcement on ROS Discourse — [discourse.openrobotics.org](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/mcap-a-recording-file-format-for-pub-sub-systems-designed-with-robotics-in-mind/24315)
- lakeFS acquired the DVC open-source project from Iterative.ai in November 2025; DVC "remains open source with its own community and website at dvc.org" — [Jisaku glossary 2026](https://jisaku.com/glossary/data-versioning-dvc-lakefs-pachyderm-deltalake-2026), [DEV Community comparison](https://dev.to/dibi8/dvc-vs-lakefs-vs-delta-lake-choosing-the-right-data-version-control-tool-for-ml-hf9)

- lakeFS documents an official Hugging Face Datasets integration — [lakeFS docs](https://docs.lakefs.io/integrations/huggingface_datasets/)
- Hugging Face Xet: deployed January 2025; in 6 months 500,000 repositories holding 20 PB moved to Xet, >1 million users; no hard cutover — repositories can hold both Xet and LFS files, with a bridge serving Xet files to LFS clients; pointer-file format kept — [HF blog: Migrating the Hub to Xet](https://huggingface.co/blog/migrating-the-hub-to-xet), [HF Xet docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/xet/overview); XetHub acquired August 2024 — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/xet-on-the-hub)
- Dec 2024 critique (pre-Xet): HF "still using Git LFS, so push times are slow and you aren't able to compare versions of your data" — [Oxen.ai](https://ghost.oxen.ai/the-best-ai-data-version-control-tools/) (now partly superseded by Xet rollout)
- Microsoft ILC 2.0 ships floor-plan metadata (raster, size, GeoJSON) alongside traces, i.e., map and traces are versioned together per site/floor — [GitHub](https://github.com/location-competition/indoor-location-competition-20)
- Rerun's rosbag2 storage plugin lets you "query and train directly from rosbags" — [ROS Discourse](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/query-and-train-directly-from-rosbags-with-a-rosbag2-storage-plugin-for-rrd/57982)

### Inferences
- A pragmatic layout for a small team: raw walk JSON → immutable MCAP (or keep JSON) per session in object storage; derived per-sensor Parquet tables for analytics; lakeFS (or DVC if the team is Git-centric) for commits/branches; each walk record carries `venue_id`, `floor_id`, `map_version` (hash of the floor GeoJSON/raster) and `app_version`/`schema_version` in its metadata so replays can pin the exact map used (this mirrors ILC 2.0 shipping GeoJSON with traces and Sensor Logger's `Metadata.csv`).
- Zarr was not covered by any source found; given the row-oriented, irregularly sampled nature of phone logs, Parquet/MCAP are the better-documented choices.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark of MCAP vs rosbag vs Parquet on size/write speed/robustness; a community thread requesting one went unanswered.
- No source on Zarr for IMU/radio logs.
- No source describing an established convention for linking sensor logs to map versions; the scheme above is an inference.

## Key Question 6: Analytics and venue-insight pipelines (open-source event pipelines; privacy-preserving aggregation)

### Takeaway
Self-hostable stacks exist (Snowplow trackers → Kafka → ClickHouse; PostHog on ClickHouse; Plausible), but licensing diverged in 2024: Snowplow's core pipeline moved to a Limited Use License forbidding production use, while ClickHouse (Apache 2.0), Plausible (AGPL-3.0) and PostHog core (MIT, with some components under other licenses) remain OSI/open. For privacy-preserving footfall/heatmaps, OpenDP now hosts both the OpenDP Library (Rust core; Python/R) and Tumult Analytics/Core (taken over Oct 2025); Google published a DP heatmap algorithm; no authoritative minimum-count threshold for footfall cells was found.

### Cited Findings
**Pipelines**
- Snowplow moved Collector, Enrich, loaders and Iglu Server to the Snowplow Limited Use License in January 2024 (previously Apache 2.0); you may read/modify but not distribute, use for a competing offering, or "run it in production"; tracking SDKs remain Apache 2.0 — [Snowplow blog](https://snowplow.io/blog/introducing-snowplow-limited-use-license), [Snowplow LUL FAQ](https://docs.snowplow.io/docs/resources/limited-use-license-faq/); community fork OpenSnowcat exists — [github.com/opensnowcat](https://github.com/opensnowcat)
- March 2026 tutorial: Snowplow trackers → Collector → Enrich → Kafka → ClickHouse for self-hosted SQL analytics, with Iglu schema validation and a TTL on the events table — [OneUptime tutorial](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-03-31-clickhouse-snowplow-event-collection/view)
- PostHog self-hosted runs on Kubernetes and requires a ClickHouse cluster; PostHog offers no customer support for self-hosted instances; self-hosted covers product analytics, session replay, feature flags, experiments, surveys and error tracking — [Apify docs on self-hosted PostHog](https://use-apify.com/docs/self-hosted/analytics/posthog), [PostHog: ClickHouse "event mansion"](https://posthog.com/blog/how-we-turned-clickhouse-into-our-eventmansion)
- ClickHouse listed as Apache 2.0; PostHog listed as MIT by one source while PostHog's own stack guide mentions a "Sustainable use license" for some component — [PostHog open-source stack](https://posthog.com/blog/open-source-stack-for-engineers), [Snowplow roundup](https://snowplow.io/blog/best-open-source-analytics-tools) (conflict unresolved; check repo)
- Plausible is AGPL-3.0, chosen so rivals cannot compete "without contributing back" — [Plausible blog](https://ingest.plausible.io/blog/open-source-licenses)
- Web-analytics "heatmaps" in these tools are click/scroll heatmaps; no result combined these tools with physical footfall heatmaps — [Open Web Analytics listing](https://mintlify.com/awesome-selfhosted/awesome-selfhosted/categories/analytics)

**Privacy-preserving aggregation**
- OpenDP took over maintenance of Tumult Analytics and Tumult Core on 2 October 2025; former Tumult staff joined OpenDP; first OpenDP-hosted release Tumult Analytics 0.20.2 (docs revamp, no new functionality); Tumult Analytics (Python, built on Spark) was used by the U.S. Census Bureau, Wikimedia Foundation and IRS — [OpenDP announcement](https://opendp.org/2025/10/02/welcoming-tumult-analytics-and-tumult-core-to-opendp), [Tumult Analytics paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04133)
- OpenDP Library: Python, R and Rust bindings, Rust core "memory safe and fast", docs at docs.opendp.org; 2025 releases OpenDP Library v0.14 and DP Wizard v0.5 (linear regression, synthetic data); a Differential Privacy Deployments Registry launched 2025 — [opendp.org](https://opendp.org), [OpenDP 2025](https://opendp.org/2025)
- Google "Differentially private heatmaps": privatises the aggregated distribution then post-processes into a heatmap; compared against a Laplace baseline — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/differentially-private-heatmaps/), [arXiv 2211.13454](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13454); companion result: any DP aggregation suffers error under KL/ℓ1 that grows with resolution (fine grids are costly)
- Federated location heatmaps under distributed DP with secure aggregation using adaptive hierarchical histograms — [arXiv 2111.02356](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02356)
- Aggregate location time series still leak membership; DP bounds an adversary's ability to tell whether a user's data was included (UCL/Turing) — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10096624/1/no_comments.pdf)
- AEPD (Spain) May 2024 Wi-Fi tracking guidance: DPIA required before deployment; anonymise quickly; do not link location data with other sources — [AEPD guidance PDF](https://www.aepd.es/guides/wi-fi-tracking-technologies-guidance-for-data-controllers.pdf), [IAPP summary](https://iapp.org/news/b/aepd-regional-dpas-issue-guidance-for-wi-fi-tracking-technology)

### Inferences
- For a small team, ClickHouse (Apache 2.0) as the warehouse with your existing backend ingestion endpoint as the collector avoids Snowplow's production-use restriction; PostHog is attractive only if you want product analytics too and can run Kubernetes + ClickHouse.
- Footfall/dwell heatmaps should be computed as DP histograms over a coarse zone grid (shop/zone level like the UPCT dataset's 69/124 zones) using OpenDP or Tumult Analytics, since DP error grows with grid resolution; this also addresses the Enschede finding (KQ7) that per-device tracking over time is the violation, not counting.

### Gaps
- No authoritative k-anonymity/minimum-cell-count threshold for footfall heatmaps found; practice of suppressing small cells is widely described but no regulator-specified number surfaced.
- PostHog's current component licensing not resolved from sources.
- Apache Kafka/Flink and RudderStack licensing/status were not covered by searches performed.

## Key Question 7: Privacy law (POPIA, GDPR precedents, app-store policies, consent UX)

### Takeaway
POPIA explicitly lists "location information" as personal information, defines consent as a "voluntary, specific and informed expression of will", requires opt-in for unsolicited electronic marketing, restricts cross-border transfers (s72), caps administrative fines at R10 million (s109) with up to 10 years' imprisonment for enforcement-notice offences (s107), and the Information Regulator has been actively fining since 2024–2026 (though no location-specific enforcement found). The key GDPR precedent is the Dutch DPA's €600,000 Enschede fine (2021): a Wi-Fi footfall counter that could follow a device across sensors over time is "tracking", a serious breach even without intent. Google Play requires a Permissions Declaration Form, demo video and a prominent in-app disclosure containing the word "location" for background location, and from 27 January 2027 apps targeting Android 17 must use the location button for one-off requests and justify `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`; Apple requires purpose strings under guideline 5.1.1(ii) and privacy manifests (required-reason APIs do not include CoreMotion).

### Cited Findings
**POPIA (South Africa)**
- Definitions (s1): consent = "any voluntary, specific and informed expression of will in terms of which permission is given for the processing of personal information"; "personal information" includes "location information" (item c); "unique identifier" = any identifier assigned to a data subject and used by a responsible party that uniquely identifies them in relation to that party; "de-identify" = delete information that identifies, can identify by a reasonably foreseeable method, or can be linked to identifying information; "operator" = processes for a responsible party under contract — [popia.co.za s1](https://popia.co.za/section-1-definitions/)
- Lawful processing requires a recognised ground (consent, legitimate interests of data subject/responsible party/third party etc.); special personal information (race, health, biometrics, etc.) generally prohibited without consent; unsolicited electronic direct marketing requires opt-in consent except existing customers for similar products with opt-out; cross-border transfer allowed only if recipient bound by substantially similar law/binding corporate rules/agreement, or consent, or contract necessity; prior authorisation from the Regulator required in prescribed cases including certain uses of unique identifiers and transfers of children's/special information abroad; security compromise notification to Regulator and data subjects "as soon as reasonably possible"; Information Officer must be registered; penalties: offence for non-compliance with an enforcement notice (fine or up to 10 years, s107), administrative fine up to R10 million (s109), civil damages (s99); page last modified 16 Feb 2026 — [DLA Piper Data Protection Laws: South Africa](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=ZA)
- POPIA "uses an opt-in model of data subject consent, i.e. consumers' consent must be obtained prior to collection or processing" — [Lex Mundi guide](https://www.lexmundi.com/guides/data-privacy-guide/jurisdictions/africa/south-africa/)
- s15(3)(d): location data held by mobile operators can be further processed to mitigate an imminent public-health threat (COVID-era guidance) — [Mondaq on COVID guidance note](https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/reporting-and-compliance/928820/covid-19-guidance-note-on-the-processing-of-personal-information)
- Regulator's Direct Marketing Guidance Note (2024/25) is advisory; telephone calls treated as electronic communications — [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/south-africa/insights/2025/Data-Protection-Guidance-Note-on-Direct-Marketing)
- A vendor guide claims the Regulator requires consent for advertising/marketing cookies including audience building and conversion tracking (not verified against Regulator documents) — [FlexyConsent 2026](https://flexyconsent.com/blog/popia-south-africa-cookie-consent-guide/)
- Sept 2026: "The Information Regulator has not yet issued specific guidance on smart wearables" (invisible data collection) — [Polity 2026-09-17](https://www.polity.org.za/article/invisible-data-collection-through-a-privacy-lens-2026-09-17)
- Enforcement record: 2,374 breaches reported in FY2024/25 (~198/month) — [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/inforeg-exposes-popia-violators-as-data-breaches-mount/kLgB17ezby5M59N4); WhatsApp enforcement notice (issued Sept 2024, public April 2025) — [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/whatsapp-enforcement-notice-popia-breaches/77829); Dept of Basic Education R5m fine (set aside by High Court Dec 2025 per one source; status disputed) — [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/powers-of-the-information-regulator-and-how-the-department-of-justice-could-have-avoided-a-r5-million-fine-2/), [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/business/872960/two-departments-nailed-with-r5-million-fines-in-south-africa/); Blouberg Municipality R500k fine pursued in court (April 2026) — [Information Regulator release](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/INFORMATION-REGULATOR-TURNS-TO-THE-COURTS-TO-FINE-BLOUBERG-LOCAL-MUNICIPALITY-FOR-CONTRAVENTION-OF-POPIA_.pdf); Central Johannesburg TVET College R500k fine (2026) — [Polity 2026-10-30](https://www.polity.org.za/article/r500-000-fine-reinforces-the-consequences-of-failing-to-act-on-popia-breaches-2026-10-30); FT RAMS Consulting direct-marketing fine reported as R200k by one source and R100k by another — [Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/south-africa-data-protection-recent-developments-and-enforcement-trends/), [Clearcomply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/popia-fines-south-africa-real-cases)

**GDPR / European precedents on indoor and Wi-Fi tracking**
- Dutch DPA fined Enschede €600,000 (announced 29 April 2021) for Wi-Fi sensors counting city-centre footfall, running May 2018 – 1 May 2020; DPA: monitoring over time which phone passes which sensor "turns counting into tracking people"; found no intent to follow individuals and no indication tracking occurred, but deploying a system that makes such tracking possible is itself a serious breach; municipality appealed — [TechXplore/AP](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-04-dutch-city-fined-wi-fi-tracking.html), [EDPB national news](https://edpb.europa.eu/news/national-news/2021/dutch-dpa-fines-municipality-wi-fi-tracking_es); Cities Today reports the DPA cited GDPR Art. 5(1)(a) and 6(1) and lack of a legal basis — [Cities Today](https://cities-today.com/?p=41972)
- AEPD (Spain) guidance for Wi-Fi tracking technologies (May 2024): DPIA required before deployment, anonymise quickly, do not link with other sources — [AEPD PDF](https://www.aepd.es/guides/wi-fi-tracking-technologies-guidance-for-data-controllers.pdf); Spanish fines for Wi-Fi tracking summarised — [LetsLaw](https://letslaw.es/en/fines-wifi-tracking/)
- UK ICO 2016 Wi-Fi location analytics guidance (under DPA 1998, pre-GDPR): applies to retailers, venues, transport hubs; recommends anonymising/truncating MACs, privacy impact assessments, notices with URL/QR codes leading to an opt-in/opt-out page where users enter their MAC — [Bird & Bird summary](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2016/uk/ico-publishes-new-guidance-on-wifi-location-analytics), [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/data-and-privacy/new-ico-guidance-on-wifi-location-analytics/), [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/wi-fi-operators-must-notify-device-users-of-potential-data-processing-before-processing-occurs-says-ico)
- Irish DPC fined Google €403m over location-data processing (reported Sept 2021/2026 in different sources; not Wi-Fi specific) — [EUobserver](https://euobserver.com/238683/irish-regulator-hits-google-with-e403m-gdpr-fine-over-invasive-location-tracking/)
- MAC randomisation in modern iOS/Android skews passive Wi-Fi visitor counts (vendor note) — [Purple.ai guide](https://www.purple.ai/en-gb/guides/wifi-data-capture-a-comprehensive-guide-to-privacy-compliance-and-best-practices)

**App-store policies**
- Google Play Location Permissions policy: background location only for core functionality with "significant benefit to users"; never solely for ads/analytics; requires Permissions Declaration Form (one declared feature, ≤30 s video of feature, disclosure and runtime prompt); prominent in-app disclosure must appear in a dialog before the runtime prompt, during normal use, describe data and use (privacy policy alone insufficient), include the word "location" and a background phrase ("background", "when the app is closed", "always in use", "when the app is not in use"), list every background feature, and add "used to provide ads" if applicable; template: "collects location data to enable … even when the app is closed or not in use"; foreground-service location only as continuation of a user-initiated action; request minimum scope (coarse over fine, foreground over background); location may never be sold; remove background location from manifest if unused — [Google Play policy 9799150](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)
- Google Play 2026 policy preview: from 27 January 2027 apps targeting Android 17 (API 37+) "must use the Android location button for one-time or infrequent location requests" and `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` requires a Play Console declaration explaining why coarse/location button is insufficient; no dated sensor-specific (accelerometer/gyro/barometer) change; from Android 16 BODY_SENSORS replaced by granular health permissions — [Google Play policy preview 16558241](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241); April 2026 blog: apps must use the location button unless they need persistent access — [Android Developers Blog April 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/04/giving-users-clearer-choice-and-everyone-a-safer-more-trusted-app-ecosystem.html?hl=es_419)
- Nov 2025 Play preview: device location is personal and sensitive user data subject to the Background Location policy; apps may not access location-protected data after it is no longer necessary — [Google Play 17033915](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17033915?hl=en)
- Apple App Review Guideline 5.1.1(ii) governs purpose strings (usage descriptions) — [Apple App Review Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-purpose-strings); example rejection where the permission alert "does not sufficiently explain" the location request — [Apple Developer Forums thread 790014](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/790014)
- Apple privacy manifests / required-reason APIs: enforced from 1 May 2024; categories are file timestamp, system boot time, disk space, active keyboard and UserDefaults — CoreMotion is not among them (Apple page fetched but only title rendered; categories confirmed from secondary sources) — [Apple: Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api), [Sonar privacy manifest guide](https://trysonar.app/blog/ios-privacy-manifest-indie-devs.md)

### Inferences
- Under POPIA your walk recorder and any positioning telemetry process "location information" (expressly personal information) plus device identifiers; the cleanest basis is the opt-in consent you already have, provided the consent dialog is specific about purposes (calibration, navigation, aggregated venue analytics) and separate from marketing consent (s69 opt-in). If raw logs are stored outside South Africa (e.g., EU/US cloud), s72 cross-border conditions apply — use an operator agreement/standard clauses or keep storage in-region.
- The Enschede decision implies that even "anonymous" footfall systems are unlawful if device-level trajectories are retained; design the analytics endpoint to drop or rotate device identifiers before storage and to publish only DP/aggregated zone counts (ties to KQ6).
- Google Play's wording rules effectively dictate your Android consent copy; since your recorder is foreground and opt-in, avoid requesting background location at all to sidestep the declaration/video process; on Android 17 use the location button for one-off fixes.
- POPIA's "de-identify" definition (reasonably foreseeable re-identification, including by linkage) means hashed MACs or persistent install IDs linked to trajectories are not de-identified; this matches the Dutch DPA's reasoning.

### Gaps
- No Information Regulator guidance note specifically on location/sensor data or indoor tracking was found; no POPIA enforcement action involving location data found.
- The Dutch DPA's own decision text (whether MAC hashing was used and rejected) could not be fetched (403); details rely on AP/EDPB summaries.
- Current (post-2016) ICO Wi-Fi analytics guidance under UK GDPR not located.
- Apple's required-reason API page did not render; the CoreMotion exclusion is from secondary sources and general knowledge and should be re-checked.
- No EDPB guideline specifically on indoor positioning was surfaced; the AEPD 2024 guidance is the most recent DPA document found.
- No empirical study of consent-UX patterns for indoor positioning apps found; only the Google Play prescriptive wording and ICO's QR/URL opt-out suggestion.

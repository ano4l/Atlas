# Commercial indoor navigation / indoor positioning landscape (status as of November 2026) and implications for Atlas (SA mall navigation + analytics + sponsored search/route ads)

Research date: 14 November 2026. Items dated before 2024 are marked "(older)". "Vendor claim" marks statements that come from a vendor's own marketing and were not independently verified. "Not published" means no public price was found.

## Key question 1: Vendors — positioning technique, hardware, SDKs (React Native?), pricing, mall customers, funding/status

### Takeaway
The market has consolidated around two camps: (a) map-first platforms (Mappedin, MapsPeople, Pointr, MazeMap, Esri, HERE) that sell per-square-metre subscriptions to venue owners and treat positioning as an add-on, and (b) positioning-first vendors (IndoorAtlas, Situm, Oriient, Mapsted, Crowd Connected, Navigine, infsoft, Favendo, Kontakt.io) that either fingerprint radio/magnetic signals or sell BLE hardware. Almost no one publishes list prices except IndoorAtlas (€349/venue/month commercial) and Crowd Connected (US$8.5k–25k/yr per venue); React Native is now supported by Mappedin, IndoorAtlas, Situm and Pointr. Several once-prominent players pivoted away from malls (Pointr → healthcare/events, Steerpath → workplace, Phunware → hospitality, Kontakt.io → hospitals, CXApp → workplace), leaving malls served mainly by Mappedin, MapsPeople (ex-Point Inside), Pointr (Gulf malls) and kiosk vendors such as ViaDirect.

### Inferences
- The only self-serve, published, commercially usable positioning price point found is IndoorAtlas Pro (€349/venue/month for ≤10,000 m²); a 100,000+ m² super-regional SA mall would fall into unpublished Enterprise tiers for every vendor.
- React Native is now mainstream for indoor SDKs (Mappedin, IndoorAtlas, Situm, Pointr), so a RN-based Atlas can integrate a third-party positioning SDK later without re-platforming.
- Hardware-free (magnetic/Wi-Fi fingerprint) vendors (IndoorAtlas, Oriient, Mapsted) still require a fingerprinting walk-through per venue; "no hardware" does not mean "no site visit".
- Vendor pivots away from malls (Pointr, Phunware, Steerpath, Kontakt.io, CXApp) and MapsPeople's slow ARR growth (+12%) suggest mall wayfinding alone has been a hard SaaS business; healthcare and workplace have been more lucrative.

### Gaps
- No published pricing for Mappedin, Pointr, Situm (beyond "from €9.5/day" for 3D maps), Navigine, Oriient, HERE, infsoft, Favendo, Mapsted, Esri IPS.
- No 2025–26 funding/status news found for IndoorAtlas, Situm, Navigine, Steerpath, Proximi.io, infsoft, Favendo; Aisle411's fate after ~2014 is undocumented in sources found.
- Dubai Mall case-study outcome metrics could not be extracted (PDF binary).

## Key question 2: Platform players — Apple, Google, Samsung, Huawei (what they offer malls and developers in 2026)

### Takeaway
Apple's Indoor Maps Program is free but gatekept (its FAQ currently says Apple Maps only adds indoor maps of airports' public areas, uses Wi-Fi-based positioning with no beacons, exposes only floor level via Core Location, and offers no wayfinding API); Google's indoor floor plans are free to submit but coverage is venue-partner driven and indoor Live View's last big expansion was Feb 2023 to ~1,000 venues in 12 cities (none in Africa); Huawei Petal Maps launched integrated indoor-outdoor navigation at HDC 2025 but coverage is China-centric; nothing found on Samsung indoor maps.

### Cited Findings
- Apple FAQ: Apple adds indoor maps "only for publicly accessible areas of airports, and only with explicit owner or operator permission"; beacons not required — sufficient 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi coverage is enough, devices need not connect but Wi-Fi must be on; IMDF files are not exposed through MapKit/MapKit JS (you overlay your own maps); Core Location and Safari expose floor level as an integer; "Apple offers no indoor wayfinding API. Third-party platforms provide that capability" (FAQ names Mappedin and Venuex). Country list includes many countries (South Africa not confirmed from fetched text). — [Apple Indoor Maps FAQ](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program/faq)
- Apple program page: no fees for registration, IMDF use, or the indoor positioning service; workflow = IMDF map → RF survey with Indoor Survey app → positioning via CoreLocation/Safari; "passive use of existing infrastructure, requires no additional hardware deployment". — [Apple Indoor Maps and Positioning](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program)
- Third-party WWDC19 note (older): program "only available for large properties with more than 5 million annual visitors". — [WWDC Notes 245](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc19-245-introducing-the-indoor-maps-program/)
- IMDF 1.0.0 became an OGC Community Standard on 23 Feb 2021 (older). — [OGC IMDF](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/)
- Apple sample code "Displaying an indoor map" decodes/renders a subset of IMDF (WWDC19 session 241, older); Apple hosts an IMDF sandbox validator. — [Apple developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit/displaying-an-indoor-map.md); [Apple additional resources](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program/additional_resources)
- No WWDC 2025 or 2026 indoor-maps/Core Location indoor announcement surfaced in search; results were all 2019–2021. — [Apple Maps & Location videos](https://developer.apple.com/videos/maps-location)
- Google indoor floor plans: venue partners (owners or authorised reps) submit and maintain floor plans; Google prioritises large high-traffic venues; a Floor Plan Marker app improves indoor location accuracy; no cost reported by third parties. ### Inferences
- Neither Apple nor Google provides a developer-facing indoor positioning + routing API that Atlas could simply call in SA malls; Apple exposes floor level only and Google's indoor Live View is consumer-app only and not deployed in Africa. Atlas must own its positioning stack or license one.
- Publishing an IMDF file to Apple is unlikely to be accepted for a mall under the FAQ's current "airports only" language, but IMDF remains a useful interchange/export format (Mappedin and Mapxus both sell IMDF export).
- Google's own ad formats (promoted pins, sponsored results, route pins) are the closest live analogue to Atlas's "sponsored search/route advertising"; the labelling confusion noted by SEO press is a UX risk to design around.

### Gaps

## Key question 3: Business-model evidence — what venues pay for; case-study numbers; sponsored search / route advertising outcomes and failures

### Takeaway
Venue operators demonstrably pay for (1) kiosk/web wayfinding sold by area, (2) footfall/dwell analytics (now shifting from Wi-Fi probes to CCTV AI in SA), and (3) asset tracking in healthcare; evidence that in-map sponsored search/pins is a durable revenue line is thin — Aisle411 (sponsored pin drops, 2012) and Point Inside StoreBoost (paid in-app search, 2015) both ended as asset sales or silence, and only Google's own Promoted Pins are clearly alive in 2025. No published mall case study with hard footfall/dwell uplift attributable to a navigation app was found.

### Cited Findings
- MapsPeople pricing logic: venue pays by surface area; blue-dot, people-flow heatmaps and location-based marketing need a third-party IPS and raise cost (older, 2019). — [MapsPeople blog](https://blog.mapspeople.com/how-much-does-an-indoor-navigation-solution-cost)
- Mappedin: subscription by square footage/metres; map-making services extra; ISV embedding priced separately. — [Mappedin Pricing](https://mappedin.com/pricing)
- IndoorAtlas sells analytics (heatmaps, paths, dwell, visitor counts) and asset tracking as separate paid modules. — [IndoorAtlas pricing](https://indooratlas.com/pricing)
- Crowd Connected published event-app positioning licences $8.5k–$25k/yr by venue area. — [Crowd Connected pricing](https://www.crowdconnected.com/pricing)
- Aisle411 sold banner ads and "sponsored pin drops" in its app (2012); Walgreens 7,800 stores; later funding $6.3M (2013); no post-2014 outcome reporting (older). — [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/17/walgreens-gets-nationwide-in-store-navigation-but-not-in-its-own-app/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisle411). A third-party case-study site claims Walgreens saw "5–10% revenue lift" with aisle411 — unverified marketing-style source. — [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/aisle411/case-study/walgreens-customer-case-study)
- Point Inside StoreBoost (2015) let retailers "promote specific products or brands in auto-complete or to the top of search results within their apps"; Lowe's and Target used StoreMode but Point Inside "declined to say if any use StoreBoost"; Lowe's sponsored products later ran via Criteo on Lowes.com (2021) rather than the in-store map; Point Inside's assets were sold to MapsPeople in 2023. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/retailers-can-now-sell-paid-search-in-their-apps-with-point-inside-storeboost-236493); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/262870/point-inside-ad-unit-boosts-brands-products-to.html); [P2PI Lowe's/Criteo](https://p2pi.com/qa-lowes-one-roof-x-criteo); [PR Newswire MapsPeople](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/mapspeople-to-acquire-activities-from-point-inside-inc-301802257.html)
- Google Promoted Pins: extended to Demand Gen (May 2025), charged per view/click; Maps ~1 billion monthly users. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-promoted-pins-maps-demand-gen-456126); [Techmagnate guide](https://www.techmagnate.com/blog/google-maps-ads/)
- Phunware hospitality white paper claims a "$2M+ annual revenue opportunity" for hotels from its app and "guest app adoption rates exceed 70%" (vendor claim, hospitality not malls). — [Phunware white paper release](https://investors.phunware.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/341/phunware-unveils-new-white-paper-demonstrating-2m-annual)
- Mall analytics academic evidence: ISPRS IJGI 2020 study of indoor positioning data from ~534,000 customers in a Beijing mall and ~59,000 in Chongqing found store location, visibility and reputation significantly affected flow density (older). — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/a9271b96363f4f6f8d26cb139e2d2238)
- Vendor marketing (Visioglobe, Navigine) asserts navigation raises dwell time/sales without before-after numbers. — [Visioglobe](https://visioglobe.com/post/indoor-wayfinding-in-shopping-malls-why-indoor-navigation-has-become-a-revenue-driver); [Navigine blog, updated 28 May 2026](https://navigine.com/blog/7-signs-poor-indoor-navigation-apps-are-killing-your-mall-s-tenant-sales/)
- SA operator evidence of what analytics is used for: Attacq (Mall of Africa) uses Wi-Fi analytics plus card-spend data from two SA banks to measure "bounce rate" (enter and leave within 30 s), retailer overlap and spend patterns to inform tenant mix; one retailer cut bounce rate substantially within two months after a layout change; analysis is on aggregate device patterns, not individuals (18 Oct 2024). — [Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/property/2024-10-18-qa-attacq-enhances-mall-of-africa-experience-with-data-analytics/)
- SA landlords are being pitched CCTV AI analytics as replacement for Wi-Fi probe counting ("WiFi tracking doesn't count people; it pings smartphones"), June 2026. — [ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/beyond-the-ping-why-cctv-analytics-is-replacing-legacy-wifi-for-sa-landlords/WnpNgM21R6l7VrGd)
- MRI Software partnered with Pretoria-based Fatti Location (Sept 2025) for footfall volumes, dwell times and movement patterns in SA shopping centres. — [MRI Software](https://www.mrisoftware.com/za/news/mri-software-partners-with-fatti-location-to-deliver-advanced-retail-analytics-to-south-african-shopping-centres/)

### Inferences
- Landlords already buy footfall/dwell data from sensor vendors (FootfallCam resellers, Fatti, CCTV AI) and from banks; an app-derived analytics product competes with these and only wins if it adds intent data (what shoppers searched for and routed to) that sensors cannot see.
- Both historical in-map ad businesses (Aisle411, Point Inside) depended on grocery/big-box retailers with product-level search; mall-level "store search" has lower query volume per venue, so sponsored search revenue per mall is likely smaller — treat this as a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- The Canal Walk case shows the mall's own CMS-managed "top locations"/deals cards are the de facto "sponsored" surface today — unpaid, operator-controlled promotion rather than a tenant-paid marketplace.

### Gaps
- No public figures for revenue earned by any mall or vendor from in-app sponsored search or route ads.
- No published before/after footfall or dwell uplift attributable to a mall navigation app.
- Why Aisle411 and Point Inside's ad units did not scale is not documented in accessible sources.

## Key question 4: Africa / South Africa — deployments, operators, device mix, data costs, local players

### Takeaway
SA's flagship malls already have wayfinding, but it is kiosk/QR web-map based (ViaDirect at Sandton City and Eastgate; Mappedin web app at Canal Walk; printed/web maps at Fourways) — none found with blue-dot positioning or a dedicated navigation app. Landlords (Hyprop, Attacq) report footfall and analytics in annual reports and use Wi-Fi/bank data, not apps. Device mix is Android-dominant (StatCounter Oct 2026: Android 81.8%, iOS 18.2%; Samsung ~51% of devices), and data is relatively expensive (≈R20.50/GB average 2025; R85 for 1 GB ad hoc at Vodacom/MTN).

- Incumbent SA wayfinding is "no-app" by design (kiosks, QR web maps); an app-based Atlas must justify the install against R85/GB data costs and Android-heavy, mid-tier device fleets — offline venue caching and small bundle sizes are competitive requirements, not nice-to-haves.
- With ~18% iOS share, iOS-only positioning (Apple Core Location indoor) would miss >80% of shoppers; Android-first positioning quality (Wi-Fi RTT/BLE/magnetic on Samsung devices) matters most.
- Landlords' analytics appetite is proven (Attacq bank-data partnerships, Hyprop foot counters, MRI/Fatti) but they are buying passive sensing; Atlas's differentiator is intent (search/route) data, and its privacy posture must match Attacq's stated aggregate-only approach.

## Key question 5: Open-source or free offerings Atlas could legitimately reuse

### Takeaway
Reusable free assets are limited to SDK sample apps (IndoorAtlas Apache-2.0 examples; Mappedin iOS samples with demo keys under Mappedin terms), Apple's IMDF spec, sandbox validator and "Displaying an indoor map" sample code, Mappedin's free Maker floor-plan tooling (sharing/embedding behind paid Plus), Crowd Connected's one-floorplan free tier, IndoorAtlas's 30-day non-production trial, and the research-grade OpenHPS framework with its public WLAN/BLE/IMU dataset. No vendor open-sources its positioning engine or its map data.

### Cited Findings
- IndoorAtlas SDK example apps (iOS, Android, Swift) on GitHub under Apache 2.0; venues must be fingerprinted via IndoorAtlas tooling. — [indooratlas/ios-sdk-examples](https://github.com/indooratlas/ios-sdk-examples)
- Mappedin GitHub iOS repo: SDK sample apps using MVF format with demo keys (not open map data). — [MappedIn/ios](https://github.com/MappedIn/ios/wiki)
- Mappedin React Native SDK is free to install from npm with trial API keys subject to Terms and Conditions; Maker vs Enterprise data-source distinction. — [npm README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mappedin/react-native-sdk@6.21.1/README.md)
- Mappedin Maker: free floor-plan upload with automatic wall/door detection and iOS room scanning; paid Plus adds shareable links/embed. — [Mappedin Maker](https://www.mappedin.com/maker); [Mappedin Plus blog](https://mappedin.com/resources/blog/introducing-mappedin-plus)
- Apple: IMDF spec (OGC Community Standard), IMDF sandbox validator, occupants spec with sample data, MapKit sample "Displaying an indoor map"; no fees for IMDF use. — [OGC IMDF](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/); [Apple additional resources](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program/additional_resources); [Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit/displaying-an-indoor-map.md); [Apple program](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program)
- A community post links a sample church IMDF archive usable in Apple's sandbox and shows MapKit JS styling of IMDF. — [Safe Software community](https://community.safe.com/data-7/how-to-use-apple-mapkit-js-to-style-and-display-apple-indoor-mapping-data-format-11655)
- Crowd Connected free plan: one floorplan; marketing pages claim unlimited commercial use/devices with no time limit (terms conflict across its own pages). — [Crowd Connected pricing](https://www.crowdconnected.com/pricing); [Comparison page](https://www.crowdconnected.com/compare/indoor-atlas-alternative/)
- IndoorAtlas Trial: free 30 days, non-production only, 10 users, 1 venue. — [IndoorAtlas pricing](https://indooratlas.com/pricing)
- OpenHPS: "open source hybrid positioning system" for developers/researchers with a public dataset of WLAN APs, BLE beacons and trajectories with IMU data (IPIN 2021, older). — [OpenHPS IPIN 2021](https://www.openhps.org/publications/2021/ipin2021)
- Academic open-source framework for smartphone indoor localisation notes "scarcity of common datasets" and mentions AnyPlace as an open-source indoor localisation application (older). — [CEUR-WS paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2061/paper6.pdf)
- Google floor-plan submission and Apple Indoor Maps registration are both free for venue owners (Apple: no fees; Google: third-party reports no cost). — [Apple program](https://register.apple.com/resources/indoor/program); [Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2803784)

### Inferences
- Atlas can adopt IMDF as its internal/interchange venue format at zero licence cost and keep an export path to Apple/Mappedin ecosystems, while owning its own routing graph (Apple provides no wayfinding API).
- Vendor "free" tiers are evaluation-only (IndoorAtlas) or single-floorplan (Crowd Connected); none permits a commercial multi-floor SA mall deployment without a paid tier.
- Public positioning datasets (OpenHPS) are useful for testing dead-reckoning/fusion code offline but are not SA-mall-specific; Atlas's own recorder/replay tooling will have to generate venue data.

### Gaps
- No open IMDF or indoor map dataset for any South African venue found.
- Mappedin Maker's exact free-tier limits (floors, venue size, API access) not published in sources found.
- Licence terms of Mappedin demo keys/trial API keys for production use not reviewed.

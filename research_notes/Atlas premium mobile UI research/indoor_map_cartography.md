# Indoor map cartography and visual design for a mobile mall map (state as of 2025-2026)

Reading note: findings marked **[fetched]** were read from the page itself; **[snippet]** means the claim comes from a search-result summary of that URL and the full text was not opened (treat as lower confidence). Anything I could not source is in Gaps, including a few well-known items I recall but did not verify in this session.

## 1. How the best indoor maps look and why (Apple, Google, Mappedin, MapsPeople, Mapbox, Esri, venue apps)

### Takeaway
The products that publish their styling converge on the same recipe: very pale, low-saturation category fills at ~0.9 opacity on an even paler corridor, thin low 3D walls rather than tall extrusions, camera-facing labels with collision/priority ranking, and icons that appear before text as you zoom. I could not source primary design documentation for Apple Maps indoor, Google Maps indoor, Pointr, or the Changi/Westfield/Dubai Mall/IKEA/Target apps, so their styling is a gap rather than a finding.

### Cited Findings
- Mapbox's custom indoor mapping guide gives an explicit example palette for room fills at `fill-opacity` 0.9: corridor `#cce4f7`, restaurants `#fde8c8`, retail `#fcd9e0`, connectors (lifts/stairs) `#e8d5f5`, inaccessible `#d6d6d6`, default `#e8e8e8`. All are very light tints (high lightness, low chroma) distinguished by hue only. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Same guide: walls are drawn as a thin `fill-extrusion` (colour `#aaaaaa`, height 3 m, `fill-extrusion-line-width` 0.3 m, opacity 0.8); it states typical ceiling height is 3-4 m and the floor slab extrusion should be only 0.1-0.3 m. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Same guide: recommended layer order bottom-to-top is clip layer, floor slab, room fills, walls, room labels, connector symbols; a `clip` layer (slightly larger than the footprint, "by a few metres") removes basemap 3D models and symbols under the venue. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Mapbox indoor data model: Facility > Structure > Floor > Room; floors carry `name` (short button label e.g. "1", "B", "G"), `z_index` (0 ground, negative below grade), `is_default`; rooms carry `class` and optional `icon`. Requires GL JS v3.4.0+ for `IndoorControl`, mobile SDKs v11.25+. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Mapbox Standard now has first-party indoor toggles: `showIndoor` and `showIndoorLabels` (mobile SDK 11.19.0 / GL JS 3.21.0), plus `colorIndoorLabelHighlight` / `colorIndoorLabelSelect` for feature-state highlight/select of indoor labels; `mapbox.indoor-v3` data "covers major airports". **[fetched]** — [Mapbox Standard API reference](https://docs.mapbox.com/map-styles/standard/api/); [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Mappedin's default label is the "Floating Label": anchored to a location, text always horizontal at a constant size, camera-facing; a minimalist pin in a customisable theme colour; text and pins show/hide by zoom; when the text cannot fit, a slightly transparent pin is shown as a hint that zooming will reveal the name ("especially useful for small spots like information desks and kiosks"). **[fetched]** — [Mappedin: Feature 101 - Floating Labels](https://www.mappedin.com/resources/blog/feature-101-floating-labels/)
- Mappedin's alternative is the "Flat Label", painted onto the unit polygon, filling available space and flipping to face the camera; floating labels ship with two presets, `darkOnLight` (default) and `lightOnDark`. **[snippet]** — [Mappedin flat labels](https://developer.mappedin.com/ios-sdk/flat-labels); [Mappedin floating labels](https://developer.mappedin.com/react-native-sdk/floating-labels)
- Mappedin icons are set per category in the editor and become visible as the user zooms in. **[snippet/fetched]** — [Mappedin: Feature 101 - Floating Labels](https://www.mappedin.com/resources/blog/feature-101-floating-labels/)
- MapsPeople (MapsIndoors) exposes styling as per-location-type "display rules": independent zoom-from/zoom-to ranges for icon, label, polygon, 2D model, 3D walls, 3D extrusion and 3D model; polygon stroke width/opacity and fill opacity; label types Text / Flat / Graphic; label text size 1-255 px, halo width and blur settable, label max width in em on Mapbox (about 1 em per character at 16 px). Max zoom is 22 on Google Maps and 25 on Mapbox. **[fetched]** — [MapsIndoors: Configuring display rules](https://docs.mapsindoors.com/cms/core-concepts/display-rules/configuring-display-rules)
- Apple Maps indoor maps (launched iOS 11, 2017) offer a floor-level selector and a "Browse" section to find stores by category; coverage is select airports and malls. Sources are 2017-2018 press, i.e. dated. **[snippet]** — [MacRumors, 2017](https://www.macrumors.com/2017/06/22/apple-maps-indoor-maps-ios-11/); [AppleInsider, 2017](https://appleinsider.com/articles/17/12/14/apple-details-indoor-maps-coverage-of-34-airports-multiple-malls)
- IMDF (Apple's format, OGC Community Standard v1.0.0, published 2021-02-18) defines the semantic vocabulary a mall map should symbolise. Unit categories include `walkway`, `opentobelow`, `nonpublic`, `restroom` (+ variants), `elevator`, `escalator`, `stairs`, `parking`, `foodservice`. Amenity categories include `atm`, `information`, `guestservices`, `restroom.family/.female/.male/.unisex/.wheelchair`, `babychanging`, `mothersroom`, `prayerroom`, `drinkingfountain`, `seating`, `parking.ev`, `wifi`. Occupant categories include `departmentstore`, `clothing.*`, `restaurant`, `fastfood`, `coffee`, `pharmacy`, `cinema`, `grocery`, `jewelry`. Openings distinguish `pedestrian` from `pedestrian.principal` (main entrances). **[fetched]** — [OGC IMDF Categories](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/Categories/index.html); [OGC IMDF overview](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/index.html)
- Google's 2023-2025 basemap refresh (relevant to Atlas's surrounding roads): roads moved from white/yellow to grey, water to teal, parks to mint; default style switched for all Maps Platform map-ID users on 25 March 2025; pins were made "rounder and more vibrant" with the stated aim of improving "scannability, consistency, and accessibility". Google published no hex values or design rationale for the palette. **[fetched/snippet]** — [Google Maps Platform: New map style](https://developers.google.com/maps/new-map-style-opt-in); [9to5Google, Nov 2023](https://9to5google.com/2023/11/29/google-maps-colors-update/)
- The refresh was criticised for reduced road-class contrast; ex-Google Maps designer Elizabeth Laraki called it "colder, less accurate and less human" while conceding major roads, traffic and trails stand out more. **[snippet]** — [NBC/CNBC coverage](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/business/money-report/google-maps-changed-colors-and-some-people-are-upset-including-a-former-designer/3171264/)
- Esri's indoor approach is "floor-aware" layers plus a floor filter: 2D maps open on the ground floor of every facility; 3D scenes show all floors by default; features with a null floor value stay visible on every floor (used for outdoor fixtures). **[snippet]** — [ArcGIS Pro: Floor-aware maps and scenes](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.5/help/data/indoors/floor-aware-maps.htm)

### Inferences
- Atlas's current approach (soft category fills, white galleries, progressive labels) already matches the published vendor recipe. The premium gap is more likely in second-order detail than in the basic scheme: stroke derived from fill, slab/wall depth cue, label ranking, icon-before-text behaviour, and state styling (highlight/select).
- Mapbox's example makes corridors a pale blue tint and stores warm tints. Atlas uses white corridors; either works, but the corridor must be the lightest or most neutral surface so the walkable network reads as the "ground" and the route line has maximum contrast on it.
- A useful pattern to copy from Mappedin: the dimmed, unlabeled pin as a "more here if you zoom" affordance for small units, instead of simply hiding them.
- Adopting IMDF category names for Atlas's internal unit/amenity taxonomy would keep symbology, filters and any future Apple Maps submission aligned.
- Google's experience is a caution: flattening hierarchy contrast in pursuit of a calm palette draws user backlash. Keep clear lightness steps between corridor, units, non-public areas and roads.

### Gaps
- No primary design documentation found for Apple Maps indoor styling (colours, 2.5D treatment, label behaviour), Google Maps indoor styling, Pointr, or the Changi, Westfield, Dubai Mall, IKEA and Target apps. Describing these needs first-hand screenshots/teardowns; I did not find attributable teardown articles in this session.
- Mappedin does not publish numeric zoom thresholds, label sizes or its label-ranking criteria; its current v6 "Mappedin Maps" 3D default theme colours were not retrieved.
- Apple's IMDF "Apple Guidance" pages (including shopping-centre guidance) and the `display_point` definition exist in the spec but were not retrieved. Unverified recollection: `display_point` is the curated point where a unit's label/icon should be placed, and Occupants are anchored via Anchor features; verify at the IMDF spec before relying on it.
- No source found for how any vendor visually emphasises anchor stores (larger labels, logos, darker fills).

## 2. Cartographic principles: figure-ground, hierarchy, colour, typography, generalisation by zoom

### Takeaway
The attributable rules are: build intellectual hierarchy first then express it with size/weight/colour; keep screen labels at or above roughly 9-10 px with at least a 2-point step between ranks; centre and letter-space area labels; use thin halos matched to the background (not white on dark); and aim for WCAG 4.5:1 text contrast. Zoom-banded display rules per feature class are the industry mechanism for generalisation.

### Cited Findings
- Axis Maps Cartography Guide: rank features by importance first ("intellectual hierarchy"), then encode it visually; promote with larger/bolder type, capitals and strong colour; demote with smaller size, tighter spacing and grey. When sizes are small, separate ranks by at least two points. Minimum type about 6-7 pt in print and 9-10 pt on screen. **[fetched]** — [Axis Maps: Labeling](https://www.axismaps.com/guide/labeling)
- Axis Maps: area labels should be visually centred, set in uppercase, with increased letter-spacing to reinforce the extent of the feature; point labels prefer above-right, then below-right, above-left, below-left; directly above/below/side "is not preferred"; never rotate labels upside-down; avoid uneven label density. **[fetched]** — [Axis Maps: Labeling](https://www.axismaps.com/guide/labeling)
- Esri guidance: web-map type should be at least 10 px cap-height. A UNBC cartography lecture adds that area names too small for their polygon should be treated as point labels, and spaced-out area labels should not exceed about four letter-heights between characters. **[snippet]** — [Esri ArcGIS blog (A. Buckley)](https://esri.com/arcgis-blog/?p=120831); [UNBC lettering lecture, 2025](https://gis.unbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/lettering2025b.pdf)
- Halos: Esri recommends thin halos (heavy halos become blocks) and matching the halo colour to the background rather than defaulting to white; Esri cites WCAG 4.5:1 text contrast as the target and halos as a practical way to reach it. **[snippet]** — [Esri ArcUser: Polishing your halo](https://www.esri.com/about/newsroom/arcuser/polishing-your-halo); [Esri ArcGIS blog](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/?p=1255592)
- Ordnance Survey: use "a small text halo or mask" only where background detail is unavoidable. ASPRS tip (2023): a 2-pt light-grey halo at 50% transparency plus +35% letter-spacing made area labels less intrusive and more legible. **[snippet]** — [OS Guide to cartography: Text on maps](https://docs.os.uk/more-than-maps/geographic-data-visualisation/guide-to-cartography/text-on-maps); [ASPRS GIS Tips, Aug 2023](https://www.asprs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/August2023_GISTT_When-Labels-and-Text-Matter.pdf)
- MapLibre enforces a hard limit: "Max text halo width is 1/4 of the font-size" (a 12 px label can have at most a 3 px halo). **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- Felt (Mamata Akella, 20 Mar 2024): derive each feature's stroke colour automatically from its fill (checking vibrancy/brightness) so outline and fill stay in one colour family; on light basemaps use label colour from the stroke with a light halo; on dark basemaps invert it: light label text with the halo taken from the stroke colour. **[fetched]** — [Felt: Cartography tips for designing web maps](https://felt.com/blog/cartography-tips-for-designing-web-maps)
- Dark-mode research (ICA abstracts, 2024 and 2026): keep the lightness contrast structure of the light scheme using complementary lightness; users read "dark-is-more" on dark backgrounds; avoid pure white (glare) and pure black (merges with background); default colour ramps need manual contrast adjustment for dark mode. **[snippet; PDFs could not be parsed]** — [Schiewe, ICA Abstracts 2024](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/7/144/2024/ica-abs-7-144-2024.pdf); [ICA Abstracts 2026](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/12/49/2026/ica-abs-12-49-2026.pdf)
- Apple HIG: do not rely on colour alone; pair colour with text labels or glyph shapes so colour-blind users can still distinguish categories. **[snippet]** — [Apple HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color.md)
- Generalisation by zoom in practice: MapsIndoors assigns separate zoom-from/zoom-to bands to icon, label, polygon, walls and extrusion for every location type. Mapbox's indoor control only appears at zoom 16+ with a registered structure in view. Mapbox Standard exposes POI label density as a 1-5 scale (default 3). **[fetched]** — [MapsIndoors display rules](https://docs.mapsindoors.com/cms/core-concepts/display-rules/configuring-display-rules); [Mapbox indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/); [Mapbox Standard API](https://docs.mapbox.com/map-styles/standard/api/)
- Collision engine design (Mappedin): labels are projected to 2D and their boxes entered into a hidden screen grid; colliders are tested against the grid, not pairwise; off-screen labels are pre-positioned; labels hold their place during pan and are re-solved only on zoom/rotate (prevents flicker). **[fetched]** — [Mappedin: Feature 101 - Floating Labels](https://www.mappedin.com/resources/blog/feature-101-floating-labels/)
- MapLibre placement tools that implement these rules: `symbol-sort-key` (lower keys placed first), `text-variable-anchor` (try anchors in order), `text-optional` (keep icon, drop text on collision), `icon-optional`, `text-padding` (default 2 px collision margin), `text-max-width` (default 10 em), `text-letter-spacing`. **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)

### Inferences
- Label ramp for Atlas (derived from the Axis Maps 9-10 px floor and 2-pt step): roughly 10-11 px for small units, 12-13 px for standard units, 14-16 px semi-bold for anchors, uppercase letter-spaced small caps for zones/wings. In React Native these are dp, so they already scale with device density.
- Mall-specific generalisation ladder (inference combining vendor zoom-band practice with the hierarchy rule): overview = building outline, wing/zone names, anchors, entrances, parking; mid = category colour plus anchor and large-unit labels plus amenity icons; detail = all unit labels, kiosks, unit numbers, door openings. Priority key order: selected/destination > route waypoints and connectors > anchors > amenities on the route > units by area.
- Follow the "pan-stable, re-solve on zoom" behaviour for Atlas's progressive labels: recompute collision when zoom crosses a threshold or gesture ends, and cross-fade (150-200 ms) rather than pop.
- For area labels in elongated units, rotating the label to the unit's long axis is consistent with Mappedin's flat-label behaviour, but keep rotation under 90 degrees so text never reads upside-down (Axis Maps rule), and fall back to a point label plus leader when the name does not fit (UNBC rule).
- Dark theme: do not invert. Keep corridors the lightest surface among dark greys (not black), lower chroma of category fills, use off-white label text with a halo taken from the unit's dark fill/stroke, and re-check the route colour for 3:1+ against corridors.
- "Stroke from fill" (Felt) is a cheap premium cue for Atlas's 141 polygons: 1 px stroke at the same hue, 10-20% darker than the fill in light mode and lighter than the fill in dark mode, instead of a uniform grey outline.

### Gaps
- No source found giving validated categorical palettes specifically for indoor maps, or testing how many unit categories users can distinguish. General guidance I recall but did not verify here: ColorBrewer qualitative schemes (Apache 2.0 licence) carry a colour-blind-safe flag and top out around 8-12 classes; the Okabe-Ito palette is the usual colour-blind-safe categorical set; Viz Palette (Meeks and Lu) simulates CVD and reports just-noticeable-difference conflicts. Verify licences and limits before citing.
- No study found on optimal label density for indoor maps on phones.
- I could not open the ICA 2024/2026 dark-mode abstracts in full; the findings above are from search summaries.
- "Designing Better Maps" (Brewer) was not consulted.

## 3. Route visualisation and camera behaviour

### Takeaway
The documented convention is a three-layer line (wide casing, main line, optional overlay), with the travelled part either recoloured or made to vanish behind the puck. Multi-floor routes are shown by vendors as a stacked/exploded floor overview with tappable connection markers. Heading-up helps self-localisation but the measured advantage is small. I found no primary source giving numeric widths, dash animation speeds or chevron specs.

### Cited Findings
- Mapbox Navigation SDK draws the route as three layers: casing at the bottom (widest, giving a border), the main route line, and a traffic line on top. **[snippet]** — [Mapbox Navigation Android: Route line](https://docs.mapbox.com/android/navigation/guides/ui-components/route-line)
- Travelled portion: the "vanishing route line" option changes the line behind the puck "to a specific color or makes it transparent"; separate properties exist for the travelled main colour and travelled casing colour (`routeLineTraveledColor`, `routeLineTraveledCasingColor`). On iOS, vanishing defaults to false. **[snippet]** — [Mapbox Navigation Android: Route line](https://docs.mapbox.com/android/navigation/guides/ui-components/route-line); [routeLineTraveledCasingColor reference](https://docs.mapbox.com/android/navigation/api/2.6.0/libnavui-maps/com.mapbox.navigation.ui.maps.route.line.model/-route-line-color-resources/route-line-traveled-casing-color.html)
- MapLibre primitives for route styling: `line-gap-width` draws a casing outside the path; `line-gradient` requires a GeoJSON source with `lineMetrics: true` and is disabled by `line-dasharray`/`line-pattern` (so a gradient-progress line and a dashed line must be separate layers); dash lengths are in multiples of line width; `line-cap`/`line-join` default to `butt`/`miter` and should be set to `round` for routes; `line-pattern` (for chevrons) needs a sprite whose width is a power of two for seamless tiling. **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- Mappedin Stacked Maps: shows the floors of a multi-floor journey as a vertical stack (floors separated by `verticalDistanceBetweenMaps`, examples use 75 and 125 m); stack/unstack is animated; tapping a connection marker zooms to that transition; `showOverview()` returns to the whole-stack view; "X-ray paths" keep the route visible through walls; outdoor layers are hidden in stacked view. It is labelled experimental in v5. **[fetched]** — [Mappedin Web SDK v5: Stacked Maps](https://developer.mappedin.com/web-sdk/v5/stacked-maps)
- Mappedin's multi-floor navigation demo offers a toggle between Side View and Top View of the stack. **[snippet]** — [Mappedin: Multi-floor navigation gallery](https://developer.mappedin.com/gallery/multi-floor-navigation/)
- Google holds patents on stacked-floor interactions: "Floor selection on an interactive digital map" (US8464181) and expanding a 3D stack of floor maps in proportion to pinch speed. Only titles were seen; relevant as a freedom-to-operate flag for pinch-to-explode gestures. **[snippet]** — [Google Patents US8464181](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8464181)
- Orientation: a 2025 virtual-street study (Sakai and Yoshioka) found heading-up consistently gave shorter self-localisation times than north-up, especially at skewed intersections; a related paper found the advantage small after outlier removal; user-rotatable ("control-up") maps performed worst on completion time and map-viewing duration. **[snippet]** — [AHFE Open Access 2025](https://openaccess.cms-conferences.org/publications/book/979-8-950676-05-5/article/979-8-950676-05-5_8); [AHFE Open Access (related)](https://openaccess.cms-conferences.org/publications/book/978-1-964867-71-7/article/978-1-964867-71-7_8)
- A VR study found no significant difference between head-up and north-up; an older field study found physical rotation of the device most effective when a wider map is shown. **[snippet; older studies]** — [ICC 2015 proceedings](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/13/921.html); [Univ. of Bath: Comparing physical, automatic and manual map rotation](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/comparing-physical-automatic-and-manual-map-rotation-for-pedestri/)
- Burigat, Chittaro and Sioni (IEEE THMS, 2017): a third-person 3D view (camera at a fixed distance behind and above the user) beat a 2D map on orientation time before walking, pleasantness, mental demand and preference, and beat first-person 3D on orientation time, clarity, workload and preference. **[snippet; PDF blocked]** — [Univ. of Udine record](https://air.uniud.it/handle/11390/1120865)
- An 80-participant indoor-map study tested different renderings of landmarks and paths and found highlighted paths showed no sex difference in cognitive load while key landmarks affected women's load more than men's. **[snippet]** — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/ru/records/65dfad5d63b8185d9cadf701)

### Inferences
- Atlas's cobalt line fading to grey behind the user matches the Mapbox "travelled colour" convention. To look premium add: a casing (white in light mode, dark in dark mode, about 1.5-2 dp each side), round caps and joins, and keep the travelled part at reduced opacity rather than fully grey so the origin remains traceable. Suggested starting widths (my estimate, not sourced): main line 5-6 dp at navigation zoom, casing +3-4 dp total, scale with zoom within about 4-8 dp.
- Direction cue: because gradient and dash cannot share a MapLibre layer, the equivalent SVG/Skia design is two paths: a solid base, plus an overlay of sparse chevrons or a slowly animated dash offset. Keep motion slow and stop it under OS "reduce motion".
- Multi-floor: adopt the Mappedin model. Route overview = exploded stack of only the floors the route touches, with numbered connection markers (lift/escalator/stairs icon plus "to L2"); during navigation = single floor, with the off-floor remainder hidden or ghosted and a pulsing connector marker at the transition. Auto-switch floors when positioning detects the change, and confirm with a brief floor-badge animation.
- Camera: evidence supports heading-up during active navigation (fast self-localisation) with north-up/fixed "mall-up" for browsing, and argues against relying on manual rotation. A third-person tilted follow view is supported by Burigat et al. for orientation, but that was a full 3D renderer; in SVG a mild pitch is costly, so prefer 2D heading-up with look-ahead offset (user dot in the lower third).
- Treat pinch-to-explode floor gestures cautiously until the Google patents' status and scope are checked.

### Gaps
- No primary source for numeric route-line defaults (widths, casing ratios, hex colours) in Mapbox/Google/Apple; Mapbox docs point to SDK source for defaults.
- No source found on animated flow dashes/chevrons (speed, spacing) or on auto-zoom at turns, look-ahead distance, or pitch values for pedestrian/indoor navigation cameras.
- No user study found comparing travelled-vs-remaining styling variants, or stacked/exploded views vs. per-floor switching.
- Burigat et al. participant count and effect sizes not retrieved (PDF returned 403).

## 4. Position marker and honest uncertainty display

### Takeaway
Research on blue-dot uncertainty favours a uniform, hard-edged accuracy circle over a Gaussian fade for accurate interpretation, but warns users assume they are at the centre. Google's convention separates the two uncertainties: circle for position, beam width for heading. In a degraded-signal field test, snapping uncertainty to the path network (colouring the street segment) was preferred and lower-workload.

### Cited Findings
- Google Maps (Sept 2016) replaced the heading arrow with a beam: "the narrower the beam, the more accurate the direction"; a wider beam signals a temporarily uncalibrated compass (e.g. near metal or while charging), remedied by a figure-8 gesture. **[snippet of Google's own post]** — [Google blog, 2016](https://blog.google/products/maps/always-know-which-way-youre-headed-with/)
- Hegarty, Friedman, Boone and Barrett (2016, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, DOI 10.1037/xap0000103; two experiments, between- and within-subjects): given a single uncertainty glyph, people tend to assume the true location is at the centre of the region; when combining two estimates they weight the more certain one more; "the best visualization is task dependent". **[snippet; PDF not parseable]** — [Hegarty et al. 2016 PDF](http://www.ualberta.ca/en/psychology/media-library/pdfs/news-and-events/news/hegarty-friedman-boone-barrett-16jepapp.pdf)
- Barrett et al. (2015, Cognitive Science Society proceedings): people use two heuristics, distance to the nearest circle and circle size; faded (graded) circles biased people toward the distance heuristic, while uniform circles pushed them toward the size heuristic and produced more accurate judgements. **[snippet]** — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6s24q1xf)
- A 2016 IJGIS study using the blue-dot framing found the uniform blue circle matched actual probability density most closely and drew the fastest responses compared with a Gaussian fade. **[snippet]** — [ASU research portal record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/assessing-the-effectiveness-of-different-visualizations-for-judgm/)
- A pedestrian field study under degraded GPS compared three uncertainty visualisations: no difference in positional-assessment accuracy, but a street-colouring display (uncertainty mapped onto the street segment) had lower workload and was preferred. **[snippet]** — [Pedestrian navigation with degraded GPS signal](https://typeset.io/pdf/pedestrian-navigation-with-degraded-gps-signal-investigating-4rod9lds1m.pdf)

### Inferences
- Use a flat-tint accuracy disc with a faint crisp edge (not a soft radial glow) sized to the positioning system's actual error radius, and a separate heading cone whose angular width tracks heading confidence; hide the cone entirely when heading is unknown rather than showing a guess.
- For Atlas's dead-reckoning/checkpoint positioning, the "street-colouring" result translates directly: when confidence is low, snap the dot to the corridor graph and tint the probable corridor segment instead of drawing a large disc that spills across store polygons (which implies the user may be inside a shop).
- Counter centre bias with honest state changes rather than alarm: desaturate the dot from cobalt to grey-blue, drop the pulse, and show a one-line cue such as "Approximate location, tap to confirm where you are", pairing with Atlas's checkpoint confirmation flow.
- Reserve pulsing for "live and confident"; a pulsing halo on an uncertain fix reads as certainty.

### Gaps
- No study found on pulsing behaviour, dot size, or on indoor-specific (BLE/PDR) uncertainty display; evidence is from outdoor GPS-like displays and lab tasks (2015-2016, i.e. older).
- Apple's current MapKit/Core Location user-location styling specs (dot diameter, halo, heading cone) were not retrieved.
- Droid-Life (2016) described the beam width as location accuracy, conflicting with Google's own statement that it is direction accuracy; Google's post is the authority. — [Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2016/09/20/blue-dot-google-maps/)

## 5. Floor switcher and vertical context

### Takeaway
Documented conventions: short floor labels ("1", "G", "B") sorted by vertical index in a vertical button stack near a bottom or top-right corner, shown only when a venue is in view and zoomed in, defaulting to the ground floor, with venue-level features persisting across floors.

### Cited Findings
- Mapbox `IndoorControl`: appears only when a registered structure is in view at zoom 16 or higher and hides when the user pans/zooms away; button label is the floor's short `name`, with `description` as tooltip/subtitle; floors sorted by `z_index`; example places it bottom-right; `is_default` marks the initial floor. On mobile SDKs you build your own switcher and swap `floor_id` filters. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Overlapping floors must be declared (`conflicted_floor_ids`, both directions) or they render on top of each other; relevant once Atlas has partial mezzanines. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Apple Maps: floor selector reported at the top right on iPhone (2017-era coverage) and as a floor button at the bottom of the window on Mac (current support page); placements differ by platform/OS version. **[snippet]** — [Apple Maps User Guide (Mac)](https://support.apple.com/en-kz/guide/maps/mpsad299abef/3.0/mac/15.0); [MacRumors 2017](https://www.macrumors.com/2017/06/22/apple-maps-indoor-maps-ios-11/)
- Esri floor filter: 2D maps default to ground floor; null-floor features (outdoor fixtures) are always visible; a mismatched floor value hides the feature entirely. **[snippet]** — [ArcGIS Pro: Floor-aware maps](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.5/help/data/indoors/floor-aware-maps.htm)
- Mappedin animates stack/unstack between single-floor and multi-floor views and uses connection markers as the tap target for moving between floors. **[fetched]** — [Mappedin Stacked Maps](https://developer.mappedin.com/web-sdk/v5/stacked-maps)
- Li and Giudice (2013, ACM MapInteract; virtual multi-level building; preliminary): 2D and 3D maps both improved pointing and vertical-navigation accuracy over no map, with no difference between them; 2D was more accurate for the cross-floor "drilling" task (knowing what is directly above/below). **[fetched summary page; older study]** — [UMaine VEMI Lab](https://umaine.edu/vemi/?p=1238)

### Inferences
- Design for Atlas's planned multi-floor: vertical pill stack on the right edge within thumb reach, highest floor at top, short labels, current floor filled cobalt. Add status dots: one for "you are here" floor, one for the destination floor, and a thin route tick on every floor the route crosses; show result counts per floor during search.
- Keep roads, building outline and entrances as floor-independent context (Esri's null-floor rule), dimmed when a non-ground floor is active.
- Li and Giudice suggest users struggle with vertical alignment across floors regardless of 2D/3D; keep the camera position and zoom fixed when switching floors and animate a short vertical cross-fade/slide (direction matching up/down) so alignment is preserved, and ghost the footprint of the floor below under voids (`opentobelow`).

### Gaps
- No comparative user study found on floor-switcher designs (stack vs. picker vs. slider) or transition animations.
- No retrievable documentation of how Google Maps indoor or Apple Maps badge floors containing search results.

## 6. 2.5D / 3D: when it helps, cost, and how to implement or fake it

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and mostly older: a tilted third-person 3D view improved orientation time and preference in one mobile study (2017), while 2D was equal or better for spatial learning (2013). Vendors keep indoor extrusion shallow: thin walls at around 3 m with opacity, and a 0.1-0.3 m slab. For an SVG renderer the practical choice is faked depth (offset shadow/stroke), not true pitch.

### Cited Findings
- Burigat, Chittaro and Sioni (2017, IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems, "Mobile Three-Dimensional Maps for Wayfinding in Large and Complex Buildings"): third-person 3D vs 2D gave shorter orientation time before walking, better pleasantness, lower mental demand, higher preference; first-person 3D was worse than third-person. The authors note 2D-vs-3D indoor comparisons "have been rare". **[snippet]** — [Univ. of Udine record](https://air.uniud.it/handle/11390/1120865)
- Li and Giudice (2013): no 2D/3D difference for pointing and vertical navigation; 2D better for cross-floor drilling accuracy. **[fetched summary; older]** — [UMaine VEMI Lab](https://umaine.edu/vemi/?p=1238)
- A lab study of general-population users found the 3D group less effective than 2D on map reading and memorising tasks; earlier work suggested mobile 2D maps allow faster initial orientation and route finding. **[snippet]** — [Cartography and Geographic Information Science, 2023](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/15230406.2023.2183525)
- Vendor (non-rigorous) focus-group note: 3D floor plans give a good overview, but users find it hard to see where they are and to link the map to the real building. **[snippet; vendor blog]** — [3D Wayfinder](https://webdev.3dwayfinder.com/?p=2234)
- MapLibre's official indoor example extrudes rooms from GeoJSON `height`/`base_height`/`color` properties with `fill-extrusion-opacity: 0.5` so interiors remain visible through walls. **[snippet]** — [MapLibre: Extrude polygons for 3D indoor mapping](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/3d-extrusion-floorplan/)
- MapLibre `fill-extrusion` constraints: `fill-extrusion-height`, `-base` and `-color` support feature-state; `fill-extrusion-opacity` is per layer only (no per-feature opacity; alpha in colour is ignored); `fill-extrusion-vertical-gradient` defaults to true (sides darken toward the base, a free ambient-occlusion cue); negative heights are unsupported. **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- Mapbox recommends walls 3 m high, 0.3 m thick at 0.8 opacity over flat room fills, with a 0.1-0.3 m slab: depth comes from walls and slab, not from extruding whole units. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox: Custom indoor mapping](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/indoor-mapping/)
- Mapbox Standard's `show3dObjects` bundles shadows and ambient occlusion; `lightPreset` (dawn/day/dusk/night) changes colour temperature and shadow direction; `theme` offers `default`, `faded`, `monochrome`. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox Standard API](https://docs.mapbox.com/map-styles/standard/api/)

### Inferences
- For Atlas today (custom SVG): fake 2.5D rather than pitch. Options in increasing cost: (a) a 1-2 dp darker "lip" path offset down-right under each unit; (b) one blurred drop shadow under the whole building footprint to lift it off the roads (single path, cheap); (c) selected-unit "lift" (offset the unit 2-3 dp up-left, add shadow, raise fill saturation). Per-unit blurred shadows on 141 polygons in react-native-svg are likely to hurt pan/zoom frame rate; do them as a pre-merged layer or move to Skia.
- If Atlas later moves to MapLibre Native, use the Mapbox recipe (flat fills, thin wall extrusions, shallow slab, max pitch around 45-50 degrees) and feature-state driven height/colour for the selected unit; keep labels viewport-aligned.
- Given mixed evidence, 2D should stay the default for browsing and search (spatial learning, cross-floor alignment), with tilt reserved for the route overview/stacked-floor view, where the 3D advantage (orientation before walking) was measured.

### Gaps
- No quantitative mobile performance data found for fill-extrusion vs flat fills, or for react-native-svg vs React Native Skia at this polygon count.
- No recent (2023+) controlled study of 2.5D extruded mall maps on phones found; core evidence is 2013-2017.
- The 2023 CaGIS study's stimulus (indoor or outdoor) was not verified from full text.

## 7. Landmarks and brand logos

### Takeaway
Landmarks demonstrably matter for indoor route-following but salience depends on user familiarity and landmark position at decision points. Logos on maps are commercially common and arguably nominative use, but there is no direct ruling for wayfinding apps; permission via the mall/tenant agreement is the safe route.

### Cited Findings
- Indoor landmark salience varies with wayfinder familiarity, and ML-derived salience weights differ from what participants report. **[snippet]** — [eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/6tt8j58m)
- Indoor landmark attributes differ significantly from outdoor ones, so outdoor selection methods cannot be reused directly; an indoor landmark navigation model can auto-generate landmark-based route instructions. **[snippet]** — [Geografie, 2019](https://geografie.cz/media/pdf/geo_2019124020103.pdf)
- Position at the intersection matters: four experiments studied which corner landmark people choose for route instructions with visual/semantic salience held equal (Hamburger, Roser and Knauff, 2022). **[snippet]** — [Frontiers in Computer Science, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2022.1044151)
- A study with 74 participants navigating an indoor route produced a dataset for predicting indoor landmark salience from images; automated indoor salience prediction is described as an emerging area with few prior approaches. **[snippet]** — [Austrian Academy of Sciences Press](https://austriaca.at/0xc1aa5576_0x00369cc2.pdf)
- University of Zurich GIVA group has work on the design of landmarks on mobile maps (title only seen). **[snippet]** — [UZH GIVA](https://geo.uzh.ch/en/units/giva/news0/design-of-landmarks-on-a-mobile-map.html)
- Mapbox Standard ships hand-drawn landmark icons (`showLandmarkIcons`, off by default), reported at 6,500 icons across 450 cities with redesigned night-mode styling: evidence that pictorial landmarks are a current premium-map trend. **[fetched/snippet]** — [Mapbox Standard API](https://docs.mapbox.com/map-styles/standard/api/); [Mapbox blog](https://mapbox.com/blog/detailed-architecture-and-new-design-flexibility-enhance-the-latest-release-of-mapbox-standard)
- Logo-first wayfinding exists commercially (e.g. a touchscreen directory at Abu Dhabi Mall that opens with a cloud of logos). **[snippet]** — [Sixteen:Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=14039)
- Legal: Regional Court of Cologne (LG Koln, 7 Mar 2017) held that showing a protected logo on a trade-fair site map to locate a stand was not trademark use and dismissed the claim (German law; law-firm summary). **[snippet]** — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/depiction-of-a-protected-trademark-on-the-site-map-for-a-fair-not-a-trademark-violation)
- US nominative fair use permits using a mark to identify the owner's goods/services where no sponsorship or endorsement is implied, but practitioners caution "do not assume you are permitted to use another company's logo"; using the logo (vs. plain name) is the riskier element. **[snippet]** — [DuetsBlog](https://www.duetsblog.com/2020/03/articles/advertising/nominative-fair-use-of-anothers-logo/); [Fish and Richardson](https://www.fr.com/third-party-trademarks-fair-use-or-foul/); [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/when-you-need-permission-use-trademarks.html)

### Inferences
- Use logos selectively: anchors and major brands at mid zoom (where they serve as landmarks), in a uniform white rounded badge of fixed size (about 24-32 dp) so heterogeneous logos do not break the calm palette; everyone else gets category glyph plus text. Always keep the text name available (accessibility, search, colour-blind users).
- In turn instructions, reference landmarks at decision points and on the turn side ("turn left at Woolworths") and visually emphasise those units along the active route (stronger fill, pinned label).
- Because Atlas's users include both first-time and regular visitors, prefer landmarks with high semantic salience for novices (anchors, food court, escalators, entrances) over small idiosyncratic shops.
- Licensing path: obtain logo rights through the mall operator's tenant marketing agreements or direct brand permission; do not rely on fair-use arguments, and get local (South African, if applicable) legal advice because the sources here are German and US.

### Gaps
- No ruling or guidance found specific to logos in indoor navigation apps, and nothing on South African or other non-US/DE trademark law.
- No study found measuring logo vs text label legibility/recognition on mobile indoor maps.
- No evidence gathered on photo or Street-View-style aids indoors (e.g. Google indoor Live View, Mappedin imagery).

## 8. Styling tools and open resources (with licences)

### Takeaway
MapLibre's style spec provides all the primitives needed for a premium indoor style (feature-state on colour/halo/extrusion, sort keys, variable anchors, casings), and CC0 icon sets (Maki, Temaki, Pinhead) plus public-domain AIGA/DOT pictograms cover amenity symbology without licence risk. ISO 7001 itself is a paid, copyrighted standard.

### Cited Findings
- MapLibre feature-state support (for hover/selected/on-route styling without re-uploading data): supported on `fill-extrusion-height`, `-base`, `-color`, `line-gap-width`, `text-color`, `text-opacity`, `icon-opacity`, `text-halo-width/-blur/-color`; not supported on layout properties (`symbol-sort-key`, `text-size`-style layout, `text-optional`) nor on `fill-extrusion-opacity` or `line-gradient`. **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- MapLibre label defaults: `text-halo-width` 0, `text-halo-color` transparent, `text-padding` 2 px, `text-max-width` 10 em, `text-letter-spacing` 0, `text-pitch-alignment` auto; `text-overlap` enum is `never`/`always`/`cooperative`. **[fetched]** — [MapLibre Style Spec: Layers](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/)
- Mapbox Standard configuration ideas worth mirroring in a custom theme system: four light presets; themes `default`/`faded`/`monochrome`/custom LUT; POI label density 1-5; POI background `circle` or `none`; POI colour mode `default` (per category) or `single`; dedicated highlight vs select colours. **[fetched]** — [Mapbox Standard API](https://docs.mapbox.com/map-styles/standard/api/)
- Maki (Mapbox): about 215 icons, listed as CC0 on Iconify (no attribution, commercial use allowed); one SourceForge mirror mentions a CC-BY licence, so confirm against the repo LICENSE. **[snippet]** — [Iconify: Maki](https://icon-sets.iconify.design/maki)
- Temaki (Bryan Housel / iD editor): "an icon expansion pack for Maki", CC0. **[snippet]** — [Temaki on GitHub](https://github.com/tyrasd/temaki); [Iconify: Temaki](https://icon-sets.iconify.design/temaki/)
- Pinhead: public-domain (CC0) standardised map icons consolidating Maki, Temaki and other sets. **[snippet]** — [Pinhead on GitHub](https://github.com/dschep/pinhead)
- Roentgen icon set is CC BY (attribution required): do not mix in without attribution. **[snippet]** — [ecosyste.ms: Roentgen](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fenzet%2Froentgen)
- AIGA/DOT Symbol Signs: 50 passenger/pedestrian pictograms "available for all to use, free of charge" (EPS/GIF); SEGD describes them as copyright-free and public domain. Caveat: the Red Cross objected to the first-aid red cross symbol in the set. **[snippet]** — [AIGA: Symbol Signs](https://aiga.org/resources/symbol-signs); [SEGD](https://segd.org/?p=22932)
- ISO 7001:2023 (Graphical symbols: registered public information symbols) is the current edition; it is copyright-protected and sold via ISO/national bodies; it excludes safety signs. **[snippet]** — [ISO 7001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/77442.html)
- IMDF is an OGC Community Standard (v1.0.0, 2021) built on GeoJSON (RFC 7946) with an extension model: a free, open schema for venue, level, unit, opening, amenity, anchor, occupant and relationship features. **[fetched]** — [OGC IMDF](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/index.html)
- A community talk documents implementing interactive indoor maps with MapLibre and IMDF (slides only; not opened). **[snippet]** — [Speaker Deck](https://speakerdeck.com/kyotonagoya/implementing-interactive-indoor-maps-with-maplibre-and-imdf)

### Inferences
- Even while Atlas stays on custom SVG, structuring its theme as a MapLibre-like token set (per-category fill/stroke/label colours for default/highlight/selected/on-route/dimmed states, in light and dark) makes a later MapLibre migration mechanical and gives designers one table to tune.
- Amenity symbology: use Maki/Temaki/Pinhead glyphs (CC0) drawn white on a solid coloured circle or rounded-square badge at a constant 20-24 dp, with shapes echoing AIGA/ISO conventions for restrooms, lifts, escalators, stairs, information, ATM, parking. Avoid a red cross for first aid (use a green or white-on-green cross).
- Amenities are orientation-critical and small in area, so give them icon priority above ordinary unit labels at mid zoom (icon stays, text optional): the `text-optional` pattern.

### Gaps
- Maputnik, OSM Carto icons, ColorBrewer and Viz Palette were not looked up in this session. Unverified recollection: Maputnik is an MIT-licensed open-source visual editor for MapLibre styles; ColorBrewer is Apache 2.0; OSM Carto symbols are CC0. Confirm before citing.
- Whether ISO 7001 symbol designs may be redrawn and used without a licence was not confirmed by any source.
- No published end-to-end indoor style guide from Mappedin or Esri (colour tokens, type ramp) was found; Mapbox's indoor guide is the only one with concrete hex values.

## 9. Evidence base: usability studies and standards

### Takeaway
The empirical base for indoor map design on phones is thin and mostly pre-2020: one solid mobile 2D-vs-3D comparison (2017), a preliminary multi-level learning study (2013), blue-dot uncertainty experiments (2015-2016), and scattered landmark and orientation studies (2019-2025). Most specific styling numbers in circulation come from vendor documentation and cartographic convention rather than controlled indoor studies.

### Cited Findings
- 2D vs 3D (mobile, real building): Burigat, Chittaro and Sioni, IEEE THMS 2017, DOI 10.1109/THMS.2017.2693684: third-person 3D beat 2D on orientation time, pleasantness, mental demand and preference. **[snippet; older]** — [Univ. of Udine record](https://air.uniud.it/handle/11390/1120865)
- 2D vs 3D (virtual multi-level building): Li and Giudice, ACM MapInteract 2013: equal for pointing/vertical navigation; 2D better for cross-floor alignment; authors call results preliminary. **[fetched; older]** — [UMaine VEMI Lab](https://umaine.edu/vemi/?p=1238)
- Uncertainty: Hegarty et al. 2016 (JEP: Applied), Barrett et al. 2015 (CogSci), and a 2016 IJGIS study: uniform circles outperform faded ones for accuracy/speed; centre bias persists; best glyph is task-dependent. **[snippet; older]** — [Hegarty et al.](http://www.ualberta.ca/en/psychology/media-library/pdfs/news-and-events/news/hegarty-friedman-boone-barrett-16jepapp.pdf); [Barrett et al.](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6s24q1xf); [IJGIS 2016](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/assessing-the-effectiveness-of-different-visualizations-for-judgm/)
- Orientation: Sakai and Yoshioka 2025 (heading-up faster for self-localisation; manual rotation worst); null result in a 2015 VR study. **[snippet]** — [AHFE 2025](https://openaccess.cms-conferences.org/publications/book/979-8-950676-05-5/article/979-8-950676-05-5_8); [ICC 2015](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2015/papers/13/921.html)
- Landmarks and paths on indoor maps: 80-participant study on navigational efficiency and cognitive load; 74-participant indoor salience dataset; Hamburger et al. 2022 on landmark position. **[snippet]** — [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/ru/records/65dfad5d63b8185d9cadf701); [Austriaca](https://austriaca.at/0xc1aa5576_0x00369cc2.pdf); [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2022.1044151)
- A UMaine VEMI paper, "Details on demand" style mobile visual interface for indoor wayfinding, exists and is likely relevant to level-of-detail design (title only seen). **[snippet]** — [UMaine VEMI Lab](https://umaine.edu/vemi/resource/details-demand-mobile-visual-interface-facilitating-indoor-wayfinding)
- Standards: ISO 7001:2023 (public information symbols; paid), AIGA/DOT symbol signs (public domain), OGC IMDF 1.0.0 (2021; open data model), WCAG 4.5:1 text contrast as cited by Esri for map labels. **[snippet/fetched]** — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/77442.html); [AIGA](https://aiga.org/resources/symbol-signs); [OGC IMDF](https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/index.html); [Esri blog](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/?p=1255592)

### Inferences
- Because controlled evidence is sparse and old, Atlas should treat the vendor conventions as defaults and validate the contested choices itself with quick in-mall tests: heading-up vs fixed orientation, tilt on or off, low-confidence marker treatments, and label density at each zoom band. Time-to-first-step ("orientation time") is the metric the literature uses and is easy to instrument.
- Conflicts to report honestly: 3D is preferred and faster for initial orientation in one study, but 2D is equal or better for learning and map reading in others; heading-up helps in some studies and shows no effect in another.

### Gaps
- Full texts were not read for most academic items (PDFs blocked or unparseable), so sample sizes, effect sizes and stimulus details are largely missing; anything marked [snippet] should be re-verified before being quoted with numbers.
- No studies found from 2023-2026 specifically evaluating mall-app map styling (colour, labels, extrusion) on phones.
- No retrieval of ISO 7010 (safety signs), ISO 21542 or other accessibility wayfinding standards.
